1. FAMOS: Feed-Forward 3D Articulation Modeling from Sparse Observations [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Kevin Qu, Stefan Ainsworth, Jiahao Wang, Gordon Wetzstein, Qianqian Wang
   Affiliation: Stanford University; ETH Zürich
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.20817
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20817
   Summary: Modeling articulated objects from sparse monocular views is hard because each
   observation reveals only partial geometry and motion evidence. Existing feed-forward
   methods infer articulation from a single view and rely heavily on category-level shape
   priors. FAMOS predicts movable-part segmentation and joint parameters from an unordered
   sparse set of partial point clouds by jointly reasoning over multiple observations.
   The model introduces a Multi-state Articulation Transformer (MAT) with alternating
   state-wise and global attention (K=16 part queries, L=6 transformer layers, D=768),
   plus an Observed Articulation Span (OAS) objective that supervises the motion range
   each part exhibits across the input set, encouraging the model to fully leverage
   multi-view evidence. A procedural data generator synthesizes self-annotated assets to
   overcome limited dataset diversity. Evaluated on PartNet-Mobility, ACD, and
   ArtiCraft-10K, FAMOS outperforms Particulate by 8.1, 27.8, and 21.5 points respectively.

2. RetireOPD: Self-Retiring On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Yan Yu, Zhengxi Lu, Yizhou Liu, Yichen Pan, Aozhe Wang, Qipeng Chen,
            Hua Yang, Wenqi Zhang, Weiming Lu, Qianglong Chen, Yongliang Shen
   Affiliation: Zhejiang University; Alibaba Group
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.20784
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20784
   Summary: Multi-turn agents trained with reinforcement learning (RL) receive a single
   scalar reward per trajectory, motivating on-policy distillation (OPD) from a
   skill-conditioned self-teacher to supply dense token-level supervision. RetireOPD
   identifies two failure modes: privileged information does not always make a teacher
   reliable in agentic tasks, and the benefit of distillation is stage-dependent. The
   framework first trains a decoupled skill-conditioned teacher with environment rewards,
   then jointly trains a skill-free student with RL and OPD. Adaptive Retirement allows
   the student to automatically drop the teacher once their discrepancy stops shrinking
   and the student reaches a target fraction of the teacher's success rate, after which
   RL training continues alone. Across three model scales on ALFWorld, RetireOPD achieves
   +14.1–18.8% success rate over RL-only; on WebShop, +11.8–19.0%.

3. StageGuard: Learning Stage Transitions for Long-Horizon Robot Tasks via Agentic Distillation [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Wenbo Cui, Jing Xu, Rui Zhao, Yunchao Liu, Chaobin Ye, Xinyu Zhang, Mingkui Tan
   Affiliation: South China University of Technology; Tencent Robotics X
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.20791
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20791
   Summary: Hierarchical robot planning frameworks execute long-horizon tasks by chaining
   skills from multiple control policies. Determining when to terminate the current skill
   and advance to the next subtask (a stage transition) is essential but has relied on
   manually designed completion signal checkers. Large VLMs offer strong reasoning but
   have decision boundaries misaligned with task completion, and cloud deployment adds
   latency incompatible with real-time monitoring. StageGuard uses agentic distillation:
   a teacher VLM generates structured reasoning traces from demonstration trajectories
   through a three-layer reasoning architecture (state layer, task layer, decision layer),
   then a lightweight student VLM is fine-tuned on these traces to generate compact
   self-explanations for efficient online stage-transition decisions. Evaluated on
   BEHAVIOR-1K and validated on real robots, StageGuard substantially improves
   stage-transition accuracy while supporting efficient closed-loop monitoring.

4. AgriScope: Pixel-Grounded Multimodal Understanding for Agricultural Images [ICML 2026]
   Authors: Abderrahmene Boudiaf, Mohamad Alanssari, Irfan Hussain, Sajid Javed
   Affiliation: Khalifa University of Science and Technology
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.20325 — Accepted, ICML 2026
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20325
   Summary: Agricultural image understanding requires fine-grained recognition of plant
   diseases, pests, crop structures, and botanical species under complex real-world
   conditions. Existing MLLMs produce text-only outputs without pixel-level grounding,
   limiting actionability for precision agriculture. AgriScope is a unified framework
   supporting image-level, region-level, and pixel-level understanding via biological-
   semantic encoding, dense spatial representations, and a pixel decoder. It enables
   grounded caption generation, referring expression segmentation, and multi-turn
   multimodal interaction for agricultural imagery. To support training, the paper
   introduces AgriGround: 500K images and 11M instruction-following samples spanning
   plant disease, crop/weed identification, insect pest recognition, and botanical
   understanding, built via a multi-stage automatic annotation pipeline. On grounded
   caption generation, AgriScope raises CIDEr from 107.9 to 118.6 and mIoU from
   55.0 to 59.4 over the best prior baseline.

5. UFO: Chain-of-Evaluation for Omni-Condition Alignment in Multi-Modal Image Generation [ICML 2026]
   Authors: Danning Zhang, Yijing Lin, Shuhan Zhuang, Mengqi Huang, Shaojin Wu,
            Shancheng Fang, Zhendong Mao
   Affiliation: University of Science and Technology of China
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.12397 — Accepted, ICML 2026
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12397
   Summary: Multi-modal image generation models are conditioned on diverse inputs
   (text, reference images, style, layout, etc.) simultaneously, but evaluation
   methods assess each modality independently, contradicting the simultaneous alignment
   objective and producing poor agreement with human judgments. UFO introduces an
   Atomized Chain-of-Evaluation paradigm that decomposes omni-condition alignment into
   a sequential chain of fine-grained, disentangled Atomic Evaluation Units (AEUs),
   categorizes them by modality-relevance class, and calls general or dedicated
   functional verifiers for each AEU type. UFO-Bench provides 660 test cases across
   7 categories and 3 difficulty tiers designed to stress conflicting multi-modal
   conditions. UFO achieves Spearman ρ=0.6889 correlation with human rankings,
   a 15.25% average improvement over prior metrics.
