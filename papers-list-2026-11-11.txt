1. FEROM: Frontier Endogenous Reveal-Order Marginal Policy Optimization for Masked Diffusion LMs
   Authors: NeurIPS 2026 Submission (anonymous)
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2611.10369 — cs.LG / cs.CL (NeurIPS 2026; submitted 7 Nov 2026)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10369
   Summary: Standard policy gradient methods for masked diffusion language models either fix the
   unmasking schedule (introducing bias) or sample a single trajectory (introducing high variance).
   FEROM treats the reveal order as a latent variable and derives a Rao-Blackwellized
   policy-gradient identity over all possible unmasking sequences, expressing the estimator as
   posterior edge occupancy on a reveal-state directed acyclic graph. A tractable budgeted
   approximation called Frontier Reveal Marginalization (FRM) is integrated into a GRPO-style
   objective, replacing single-path log-scores with a locally marginalized, edge-based surrogate
   that jointly reduces bias and variance in diffusion-LM reinforcement learning.

2. Module-Aware Optimization for Graph Neural Networks (GraphMuon)
   Authors: Guy Hadad, Haggai Roitman, Moshe Eliasof, Bracha Shapira
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2611.08379 — cs.LG / cs.AI (NeurIPS 2026; submitted 5 Nov 2026)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08379
   Summary: The Muon optimizer orthogonalizes gradient matrices via Newton-Schulz iterations but
   ignores that GNN layer inputs are graph-propagated, amplifying updates and destabilizing
   training. GraphMuon adapts Muon's matrix update to the covariance structure of
   graph-propagated layer representations by orthogonalizing in a metric defined by those
   propagated inputs and normalizing step sizes by the update's induced effect on layer
   representations. Across 15 dataset-architecture configurations covering node classification,
   inductive graph classification, and graph regression, GraphMuon achieves 2.13-7.38x speedups
   in wall-clock time-to-target over AdamW (13 out of 15 configurations) while maintaining
   competitive or superior predictive performance.

3. VIGOR: Visual Gain Ordering for Hallucination Mitigation in Multimodal Discrete Diffusion Language Models
   Authors: NeurIPS 2026 Submission (anonymous)
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2611.05066 — cs.CV / cs.CL (NeurIPS 2026; submitted 3 Nov 2026)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05066
   Summary: Hallucination in multimodal discrete diffusion LMs (such as LLaDA-V and Lumina-DiMOO)
   stems not only from token prediction errors but also from premature commitment: some masked
   positions are unmasked too early because language priors dominate confidence scores while visual
   evidence is underutilized. VIGOR is a training-free, plug-and-play inference strategy that
   reorders masked positions by their counterfactual visual evidence: at each denoising step it
   computes a visual gain score by comparing confidence under the original image-conditioned pass
   with confidence under a visual-ablation pass, and prioritizes positions whose predictions are
   more strongly supported by the image. VIGOR consistently reduces hallucination and improves
   multimodal reasoning performance across benchmarks on both evaluated backbones.

4. Reward-Estimated Hypergradient for Bilevel Reinforcement Learning with Black-Box Follower
   Authors: NeurIPS 2026 Submission (anonymous)
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2611.11998 — cs.LG / cs.AI (NeurIPS 2026; submitted 9 Nov 2026)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11998
   Summary: Bilevel reinforcement learning with a white-box follower computes hypergradients from
   the follower's known reward, but many real applications involve a black-box follower whose
   reward is unknown. RE-HG addresses this by using inverse reinforcement learning to estimate
   the follower's reward from observed trajectories, then computing the leader hypergradient from
   the estimate. Because naive IRL introduces reward-shaping bias and numerical instability from
   ill-conditioned Hessians, RE-HG adds a bias-cancellation mechanism that aggregates information
   across diverse environmental dynamics, plus eigenvalue truncation to suppress hypergradient
   norm explosions. RE-HG produces gradient estimates aligned with a white-box oracle and
   outperforms the oracle on sharp reward landscapes where it gets stuck in local minima.

5. Scaling Laws for SAE Training Data
   Authors: Nikita Koriagin, Nikita Balagansky, Daniil Gavrilov (T-Tech)
   Venue/ID: NeurIPS 2026 Poster #148634 (also at ICML 2026)
   URL: https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/148634
   Summary: Sparse autoencoder (SAE) training is typically bottlenecked by the amount of model
   activations that must be stored. The authors show that SAE quality depends on the number of
   unique activations in the training buffer rather than on whether every training token is fresh,
   and they fit a data-constrained scaling law describing a short diversity-limited regime
   followed by a plateau where replay of the existing buffer preserves both reconstruction
   quality and interpretability. Applying the resulting buffer-sizing rule reduces activation
   storage by 8-64x with negligible quality loss. Results are validated across Llama-3.1 and
   Qwen3 models, early/middle/late layers, BatchTopK/TopK/JumpReLU SAE architectures, and
   downstream metrics including feature absorption, automated interpretability, and sparse
   probing.
