1. Can Large Language Model Agents Learn by Watching Tutorial Videos?
   Authors: Haoyu Zhao, Zafeirios Fountas, Jun Wang
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2611.01460 — cs.LG / cs.AI / cs.CL / cs.CV (submitted 1 Nov 2026)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01460
   Summary: LLM-based agents are typically trained or fine-tuned to acquire skills, but
   weight updates are expensive and block continual learning at deployment. Tutorial
   Agentic Learning (TAL) sidesteps this by converting tutorial videos into a structured
   memory that a frozen LLM reads at inference time, requiring no parameter changes.
   Videos are parsed into step-indexed, visually grounded procedural entries and stored
   in a retrieval-augmented memory bank. At inference time, relevant entries are retrieved
   and provided as in-context guidance. To evaluate this, the authors introduce VidLearn,
   a multimodal QA benchmark built from 72 chess tutorial videos spanning 12 chess
   openings. TAL performance scales with the number of tutorial videos consumed, suggesting
   that the agent genuinely integrates new procedural knowledge rather than merely
   retrieving surface-level text. The approach demonstrates that nonparametric continual
   learning from instructional video content is a viable alternative to periodic fine-tuning.

2. The Cost of Mismatch: Noise Amplification in Zeroth-Order Reinforcement Learning
   Authors: Lianmin Chen, Junbin Qiu, Chenxing Wei, Yao Shu, Kun He
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2611.03260 — cs.LG (NeurIPS 2026 Poster; submitted 2 Nov 2026)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03260
   Summary: Zeroth-order (ZO) methods for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards
   estimate policy gradients by perturbing parameters and measuring reward differences,
   avoiding backpropagation through the environment. This paper identifies a fundamental
   noise amplification effect that plagues ZO RL in the mismatched-data setting: when
   the two noisy function evaluations used to estimate a gradient are drawn from
   different distributions (e.g., different rollout seeds), the gradient variance scales
   as 1/mu^2 where mu is the perturbation scale, creating a floor that cannot be
   reduced by simply shrinking mu. The authors contrast this with Boltzmann Targeted
   SFT (BOLT), a matched-data method in which both evaluations share the same
   randomness, which cancels the amplification term. They derive ZO-BOLT, an algorithm
   that instantiates the matched-data principle for LLM fine-tuning with verifiable
   rewards, and demonstrate empirically that ZO-BOLT achieves lower gradient variance
   and faster convergence than standard ZO RL baselines across math and code tasks.
   The analysis provides a principled explanation for why naive ZO RL for LLMs is
   unstable, and suggests matched-data perturbation as a general design principle.

3. Spectral Degeneration of Self-Attention under Isotropic Score Geometry
   Authors: Mengda Li, Jianfeng Yao
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2611.02778 — cs.LG / stat.ML (NeurIPS 2026; submitted 2 Nov 2026)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02778
   Summary: Despite the empirical success of Transformers, the statistical properties
   of attention matrices — and why attention heads tend to become low-rank in deep
   models — are not well understood theoretically. This paper introduces an idealized
   spectral model of the pre-softmax score matrix under an isotropic geometry assumption,
   and derives how the softmax nonlinearity distorts the eigenspectrum. The analysis
   shows that dominant modes (large singular values) survive and are amplified by
   softmax, while the bulk of the spectrum collapses toward zero, explaining the
   empirically observed low-rank structure of attention. A key distinction between
   bidirectional and causal attention emerges: for bidirectional attention, the stable
   rank stays bounded as sequence length grows, while for causal attention it grows
   logarithmically, reflecting the asymmetric weighting that causal masking induces.
   The paper provides code for replicating all figures. Implications include theoretical
   justification for low-rank attention approximations and insights into why causal
   models may require different regularization than bidirectional ones.

4. Strategist: Designing Agentic Reasoning at Scale
   Authors: Taiming Lu, Qi Xu, Zhigang Hua, Zhuang Liu
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2611.03618 — cs.AI / cs.CL (NeurIPS 2026 Poster; submitted 2 Nov 2026)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03618
   Summary: Most multi-agent systems use fixed pipeline templates or hand-crafted prompt
   strategies, which limits generalization across tasks. Strategist is a meta-agent that
   automatically designs inference strategies for new tasks by treating each strategy as
   a reusable executable code blueprint. Given a task description and a small development
   set, Strategist retrieves related blueprints from a growing library, proposes candidate
   strategies as code, evaluates them on real samples to measure accuracy and cost, and
   revises them iteratively. All valid candidates are added to the library, enabling
   compositional reuse across tasks. Evaluated across 30 benchmarks spanning math,
   coding, science, and commonsense reasoning, Strategist beats the strongest baseline
   on 24 of 30 and achieves an average of 8.5 absolute percentage points higher accuracy
   at 48% lower inference cost. Strategies discovered by Strategist also transfer to
   model backends the meta-agent never optimized on, suggesting the blueprints capture
   general structural insights about effective reasoning rather than model-specific
   artifacts.

5. ENGINE: Endogenous Variational MultiScale Optimization for Zeroth-Order LLM Fine-Tuning
   Authors: Zhuoli Ouyang, Bowen Li, Chen Hao, Yejin Choi
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2611.03293 — cs.LG (submitted 2 Nov 2026)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03293
   Summary: Zeroth-order optimization enables LLM fine-tuning without backpropagation,
   reducing memory requirements to that of inference. However, existing ZO methods
   suffer from high variance and slow convergence because they sample perturbations in
   the full parameter space or in a simple low-rank subspace, without accounting for
   the curvature structure of the loss landscape. ENGINE (Endogenous variational
   multiScale Optimization) addresses this by adapting the Variational MultiScale (VMS)
   decomposition from computational mechanics, which separates the parameter space into
   a coarse (low-rank) component and a fine-scale residual. ENGINE uses low-rank
   perturbations to estimate the coarse gradient, then computes a closed-form subgrid
   correction that reintroduces the unresolved full-space curvature into the coarse
   update, achieving VMS-consistent gradient estimates at no additional parameter
   memory. Across NLP fine-tuning benchmarks, ENGINE achieves up to a 2.61x speedup
   in forward passes over prior ZO methods with zero additional peak memory overhead,
   and consistently outperforms LOREN and other state-of-the-art ZO baselines.
