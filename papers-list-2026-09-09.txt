1. Random Attention: Rethinking KV Cache Eviction for Efficient Reasoning [EMNLP 2026]
   Authors: Heng Wang, Jielin Qiu, Wenting Zhao, Cheng Qian, Liangwei Yang, Jiawei Han,
            Heng Ji, Silvio Savarese, Shelby Heinecke, Huan Wang
   Affiliation: Salesforce AI Research; University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign; Cornell University;
                and collaborating institutions
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.03430 — Accepted, EMNLP 2026
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03430
   Summary: KV cache compression methods for long chain-of-thought reasoning uniformly adopt
   the paradigm of scoring each cached token by importance and keeping the top scorers.
   This paper shows the scoring signal is nearly worthless: Random Attention keeps the
   prompt KV cache intact and evicts all other tokens uniformly at random within each
   attention head — computing no score at all. Across four models and six reasoning tasks,
   Random Attention matches the strongest prior evictors while serving 32–43% higher
   throughput in vLLM. Controlled experiments reveal two key mechanisms: the prompt is the
   fragile component of the cache (most prior-evictor gains stem from accidentally preserving
   it), and the reasoning trace is inherently redundant — models restate needed information
   as they work, and each attention head maintains its own copy, so random eviction retains
   sufficient redundant coverage without any scoring.

2. Beyond Language Priors: Diagnosing and Fixing Visual-Origin Hallucinations in Multimodal LLMs [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Peiyang Xu, Xiaopei Zhu, Jun Zhu, Xiaolin Hu
   Affiliation: Tsinghua University
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.00231
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00231
   Summary: Object hallucination in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) is widely
   attributed to language priors — the model relying on textual co-occurrence statistics
   rather than image content. This paper presents quantitative evidence for a complementary,
   under-explored cause: visual-origin hallucination, where hallucinations stem from
   incorrect visual feature extraction and misalignment between image and text embeddings.
   Using cosine similarity analysis and Smooth Grad-CAM entropy measurements, the authors
   show that hallucinated samples exhibit systematically lower image-text similarity and
   higher visual entropy than faithful samples. Based on this diagnosis, the paper proposes
   targeted interventions at the visual encoding stage that reduce hallucination rates
   without degrading multimodal understanding performance.

3. From Vision to Language: Investigating Causal Information Flow in Multimodal Decision-Making [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Davide Testa, Hugh Mee Wong, Alessandro Lenci, Bernardo Magnini, Albert Gatt
   Affiliation: Fondazione Bruno Kessler; University of Malta; University of Pisa;
                and collaborating institutions
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.05149
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05149
   Summary: How do vision-language models (VLMs) integrate visual information into
   language-based decisions? This paper applies layer-wise causal intervention on
   video-text attention pathways to trace cross-modal information flow in a video-based
   multiple-choice setting targeting spatial, causal, and temporal visual reasoning.
   Key findings: visual information is integrated primarily while the model processes
   candidate answer options (not the question); nouns serve as semantic anchors for
   multimodal enrichment, while verbs carry temporal relational processing; and a
   distinct fragility in temporal reasoning suggests VLMs struggle to reconstruct
   sequential visual information across frames, possibly reflecting linguistic temporal
   biases rather than true video understanding.

4. Compile by Training: Turning Natural-Language Specifications into Local Neural Functions [EMNLP 2026]
   Authors: Yuntian Deng, Pengyu Nie, Stuart Shieber
   Affiliation: Harvard University; collaborating institutions
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.04199 — EMNLP 2026 System Demonstrations
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04199
   Summary: Compile by Training (CbT) converts a natural-language specification into a
   reusable, composable neural function via a training-time compilation step rather than
   runtime inference. At compile time, teacher LLMs generate task-specific input-output
   examples; these examples are used to train a lightweight adapter for a compact
   interpreter model. The resulting neural function runs entirely locally without the
   teachers, can be stored, versioned, and composed like ordinary software, and achieves
   83.6% semantic accuracy on FuzzyBench-Hard — a benchmark where the fast
   Program-as-Weights compiler produces zero exact matches. CbT trades higher compile-time
   cost (roughly one minute vs.\ seconds) for far higher accuracy and a permanently
   reusable artifact, with practical demonstrations including website helpers and
   language-controlled 3D avatars.

5. LLaDA-Image: Building Strong Image Generators with Fully Open Training Recipes [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Chuyan Chen, Haoxing Chen, Kun Chen, Zhenglin Cheng, Long Cui, Ruishan Fang,
            Zhangxuan Gu, Zhicheng Huang, Zhenzhong Lan, Yuanting Lei, Haoquan Li,
            Jianguo Li, Rongchuan Li, Sidu Li, Tao Lin, Deyuan Liu, Jiacheng Liu,
            Lin Liu, Yuxuan Lou, Zhisheng Lu, Yuxin Ma, Shuheng Shen, Peng Sun,
            Chaoyang Wang, Hongjun Wang, Xiaomei Wang, Yongxin Wang, Chengzhang Wu,
            Hongru Wu, Jun Xie (inclusionAI)
   Affiliation: inclusionAI (Ant Group)
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.03796
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03796
   Summary: LLaDA-Image is a fully open 6B Diffusion Transformer (DiT) image generator
   unified with a frozen vision-language understanding module built on the LLaDA2.0-Mini
   diffusion language model backbone. Training follows a staged recipe: image-only
   pre-training builds a strong visual generative prior before any paired text-image data
   is introduced; mid-training then bridges to text-conditioned generation. Key engineering
   decisions include parameter-free RMSNorm throughout the DiT and the Muon optimizer for
   efficient large-scale optimization. LLaDA-Image achieves state-of-the-art scores of
   53.53 (English) and 53.38 (Chinese) on Qwen-Image-Bench and is distilled into
   LLaDA-Image-Turbo, which produces high-quality images in 2–4 sampling steps. All
   model weights, training code, and detailed recipes are publicly released.
