1. Base Models Can Reason By Taking a Cue From Training Data
   Authors: Sophie L. Wang, Amil Dravid, Rulin Shao, Kevin Farhat, Sewon Min, Alexei A. Efros
   Affiliation: UC Berkeley
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.06851
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06851
   Summary: Training data creates systematic associations between particular starting token cues
   and the reasoning behavior that follows in a base language model. Fixing a simple token cue
   such as ".\n\nOkay" at the start of a base model's response raises Olmo-3-7B's MATH-500 pass@1
   accuracy from 42% to 78%, and "Alright," raises Qwen3-14B's from 72% to 87%—competitive with
   RL-trained counterparts. Through causal data interventions, the paper shows that an arbitrary
   word can be turned into an effective reasoning cue, or an existing cue's effect removed, by
   modifying the training data directly.

2. LESSER: Post-Training Data Selection with Output-Layer Gradients
   Authors: Lyuxin David Zhang, Eric Wong, Surbhi Goel, Anton Xue
   Affiliation: University of Pennsylvania
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.03702
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03702
   Summary: Gradient-based post-training data selection requires expensive full-backward passes on
   every candidate sample. LESSER shows that output-layer gradients serve as a highly effective
   proxy: it is a drop-in wrapper that reduces feature-extraction FLOP cost by 9.7× for SFT and
   3.0× for RL benchmarks while tracking full-gradient selection performance on downstream tasks.
   Even when output-layer and full gradients rank individual samples differently, they consistently
   select batches with well-aligned gradients, explaining why the batch-level quality is preserved.

3. S2PD: Serial-to-Parallel Diffusion for Physically and Logically Consistent Video Generation
   Authors: Jeffrey Hu, Daniel Olmeda Reino, Ayush Tewari
   Affiliation: Massachusetts Institute of Technology
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.06847
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06847
   Summary: Bidirectional video diffusion models continue to violate physical laws and symbolic
   rules even when trained on unlimited in-distribution data from procedural generators—a problem
   identified as the "seriality gap." S2PD (Serial-to-Parallel Diffusion) resolves this by
   performing autoregressive diffusion at high noise levels (where serial computation is needed
   to coordinate interdependent events) before switching to parallel diffusion at low noise (for
   joint refinement and sampling efficiency). Across games, physical simulations, and real video,
   S2PD follows rules more reliably than bidirectional baselines and generates videos with greater
   temporal stability and better sampling efficiency than fully serial methods.

4. CLIFT: Conformal Self-Verification for Web Agent Training and Test-Time Scaling
   Authors: Yifan Zhang, Yutong Dai, Viraj Prabhu, Zhiyuan Hu, Ran Xu, Zeyuan Chen
   Affiliation: Georgia Institute of Technology; University of Texas at Austin
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.06829
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06829
   Summary: Open-source web agents trained with RL face a supervision gap: binary task success
   is too sparse for credit assignment, while frontier LM judges are too expensive to call at
   every step and cannot be assumed available at deployment. CLIFT introduces conformal
   self-verification—turning natural-language verification questions into a conformally certified
   URL-aware verifier that provides non-negative per-step evidence during training. The same
   frozen verifier bank is then used for Conformal Test-time Scaling (CTS), enabling reliable
   inference-time scaling without calling an external oracle.

5. H-JEPA: End-to-End Learning of Hierarchical World Models for Visual Planning
   Authors: Wancong Zhang, Basile Terver, Michael Rabbat, Yann LeCun, Randall Balestriero
   Affiliation: Meta AI; New York University
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.06805
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06805
   Summary: Long-horizon planning requires reasoning across timescales and abstraction levels.
   H-JEPA is an end-to-end recipe for training a hierarchy of action-conditioned JEPAs in which
   each level predicts farther ahead in its own learned latent space. When data factors evolve
   at separated timescales, higher levels learn to discard fast, unpredictable detail and retain
   slower task-relevant state. Across four simulated navigation and manipulation environments,
   hierarchical planning outperforms a flat JEPA baseline; on Visual AntMaze, a three-level
   hierarchy raises success from 18% to 73% using less planner compute. With inverse-dynamics
   supervision the approach generalizes to real-robot videos from the DROID dataset.
