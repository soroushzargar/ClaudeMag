1. Frontier Language Models Struggle to Copy: Text Can Be Better Viewed in 2D [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Haodong Wen, Yiran Zhang, Yingfa Chen, Kaifeng Lyu
   Affiliation: Tsinghua University
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2607.16072
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16072
   Summary: Even frontier language models fail to copy long input strings exactly, despite solving
   complex reasoning tasks within the same context window. The authors attribute this to a structural
   flaw in standard 1D positional encodings (RoPE): producing output token y_k from input x_k
   requires matching a relative offset that changes with input length, so copying relies on fragile
   local-context shortcuts rather than positional precision. 2D-RoPE organizes tokens into a 2D grid,
   assigning each a row ID and column ID, so copying reduces to retrieving input tokens at a fixed
   column offset. Shallow Transformers trained with 2D-RoPE achieve perfect copying at input lengths
   hundreds of times beyond training range, and the advantage holds in large-scale pretraining up to
   1.4B parameters.

2. MIRROR: Learning from the Other View for Multi-Modal Reasoning [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Wen Ye, Yuxiao Qu, Aviral Kumar, Xuezhe Ma
   Affiliation: University of Southern California; Carnegie Mellon University
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2607.21552
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21552
   Summary: Vision-language models underperform on visual reasoning even for geometry problems that
   admit equivalent text, diagram, and combined text+diagram views. Different views expose
   complementary reasoning paths: a model may solve a problem from text but fail on the diagram, or
   succeed visually while failing textually. The authors construct ODA-Data, a paired multimodal
   geometry dataset with text-dominant, image-dominant, and combined image+text views, and develop
   Modality-Informed Reciprocal Reasoning Optimization (MIRROR), a reinforcement learning approach
   that trains a model on the view where it performs best, then uses that signal to improve
   performance on the harder view through reciprocal cross-modal reward shaping.

3. DiFA: Inference-Time Forward-Process Alignment for Diffusion Models [ICML 2026 / arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Shigui Li, Delu Zeng
   Affiliation: South China University of Technology
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2607.17972 (ICML 2026)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17972
   Summary: Standard diffusion model inference treats each denoising step independently as a
   numerical integration problem, ignoring the statistical structure of the forward process. DiFA
   reframes inference-time data prediction refinement as a sequential state estimation problem,
   treating iterative predictions along the reverse trajectory as correlated observations. A
   forward-aligned temporal consensus, inspired by Kalman filtering, aggregates historical predictions
   according to structural consistency and noise-level compatibility. A deviation guidance mechanism
   counteracts over-smoothing. DiFA yields significant FID/IS/FD-DINOv2 improvements on CIFAR-10
   and ImageNet, is training-free, and can wrap any existing sampler.

4. SEED: Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Jinyang Wu, Shuo Yang, Zhengxi Lu, Fan Zhang, Yuhao Shen, Lang Feng, Haoran Luo,
            Zheng Lian, Shuai Zhang, Zhengqi Wen, Jianhua Tao
   Affiliation: University of Science and Technology of China
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2607.14777
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14777
   Summary: Outcome-based RL for long-horizon agentic tasks provides only sparse trajectory-level
   rewards, leaving a supervision gap between episode outcomes and token-level policy learning.
   SEED converts completed on-policy trajectories into natural-language hindsight skills capturing
   reusable workflows, decisive observations, and failure-avoidance rules, then distills these skills
   back into the policy. One policy checkpoint plays dual roles: acting in the environment and
   analyzing its own completed trajectories to generate hindsight supervision. After each update, the
   improved policy becomes the next analyzer, so decision policy and hindsight supervision co-evolve,
   yielding consistent improvements over static distillation on AlfWorld and WebArena.

5. See like a Robot: Robot-Centric Pointmaps for Vision-Language-Action Models [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Byungkun Lee, Dongyoon Hwang, Dongjin Kim, Hojoon Lee, Minho Park, Jaegul Choo
   Affiliation: KAIST AI; Holiday Robotics
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2607.11498
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11498
   Summary: Vision-language-action (VLA) models predict actions in the robot's 3D coordinate frame
   while observing the scene in the camera frame, creating a frame mismatch that is benign under
   fixed viewpoints but harmful when large-scale datasets aggregate demonstrations across diverse
   camera setups. Robot-centric pointmaps bridge this gap: images whose pixels store the 3D
   coordinates of scene points in the robot frame, providing robot-frame geometry while preserving
   the dense H×W grid expected by pretrained 2D VLAs, allowing integration with minimal
   architectural change. The method achieves strong generalization across camera viewpoints
   and outperforms camera-frame baselines on cross-viewpoint evaluation benchmarks.
