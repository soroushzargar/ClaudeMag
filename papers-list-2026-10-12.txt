1. HRIL: Learning Multimodal Synergy via Higher-Order Tensor Modeling
   Authors: Qun Dai, Zhao Kang, et al.
   Venue/ID: NeurIPS 2026 · arXiv:2610.12393
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12393
   Summary: Multimodal contrastive learning fails to capture synergistic effects that arise
   only from the joint combination of three or more modalities. HRIL addresses this by building
   a cross-moment tensor over modality embeddings, applying Tucker decomposition to obtain a
   compact core, and adding a regulariser that prevents the core from collapsing to a low-rank
   diagonal. The framework is evaluated on multimodal tasks where synergistic interactions
   dominate, reporting gains over established multimodal contrastive baselines and marking
   a NeurIPS 2026 acceptance.

2. RouterInterp: Understanding Superposed Specialisation in Mixture of Experts Routing
   Authors: Ilya Lasy, Nora Yinuo Cai, Kola Ayonrinde
   Venue/ID: ICML 2026 · arXiv:2610.11775
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11775
   Summary: The conventional view that each expert in an MoE network handles a single domain
   is challenged by the Superposed Specialisation Hypothesis (SSH): experts activate on
   disjoint unions of fine-grained features. RouterInterp uses Sparse Autoencoder (SAE)
   features most predictive of routing decisions and produces unified natural-language
   explanations of expert roles. On gpt-oss-20b, RouterInterp achieves roughly 65 % higher
   routing-detection accuracy than prior token-statistics-based methods.

3. Differentiable Systematic Resampling for Variational Sequential Monte Carlo
   Authors: Fredrik Cumlin, Saikat Chatterjee
   Venue/ID: NeurIPS 2026 · arXiv:2610.12094
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12094
   Summary: Systematic resampling is the most variance-efficient resampling scheme for
   particle filters, but its step-function CDF blocks gradient flow. Differentiable Systematic
   Resampling (DSR) softens the CDF-level ancestor assignment with sigmoid functions and
   sorts particles with a Morton space-filling curve so spatially proximate particles remain
   adjacent in CDF order. The bias vanishes exponentially as the temperature falls to zero.
   DSR avoids the iterative solvers of optimal-transport alternatives and achieves comparable
   or better performance on stochastic dynamical-system and handwriting datasets.

4. When Has a Bayesian Neural Network Sampled Enough? Adaptive Inference Time with Statistical Guarantees
   Authors: Fabian Denoodt, Sibylle Hess
   Venue/ID: NeurIPS 2026 Workshop · arXiv:2610.12212
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12212
   Summary: Bayesian neural network predictions are typically approximated with a fixed Monte
   Carlo sample budget, leaving the finite-sample error uncontrolled. This paper uses
   confidence sequences—anytime-valid probability bounds—to decide per-input when enough
   samples have been drawn. Sampling terminates once the relevant decision (class identity,
   full predictive distribution, or a probability threshold) can be resolved with the
   desired statistical guarantee. The method assigns more samples to ambiguous inputs,
   lowers overall inference latency, and provides a formal guarantee per prediction.

5. Iris-3B: Going Beyond the Latent with Pixel-Space Diffusion Training, Conversion and Fine-Tuning
   Authors: Hanqiu Li Cai, Chema Garabito
   Affiliation: Speridlabs
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.09450
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09450
   Summary: Latent diffusion models encode images into a compressed representation before
   denoising, introducing VAE reconstruction artefacts that may harm pixel-level tasks.
   Iris-3B is a 3-billion-parameter text-to-image model trained directly in pixel space via
   a 256→512→1024 resolution curriculum. It matches latent FLUX.2 Klein on GenEval (0.798)
   and DPG-Bench (86.52 at 1024²). A second experiment converts a pre-trained latent model
   to pixel space; neither route outperforms the latent baseline on fine-grained tasks,
   suggesting that the VAE bottleneck is less limiting than assumed.
