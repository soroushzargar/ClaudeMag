1. TRIAD: Benchmarking Omni-Modal Ambiguity in Multimodal Large Language Models
   Authors: Zhaolu Kang, Yidi Wang, Yile Li, Siqi Zeng, Yingjie He, Richeng Xuan, Zhichao Hu
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.30841 — NeurIPS 2026 (Datasets and Benchmarks Track)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30841
   Summary: Current omni-modal benchmarks test whether MLLMs can answer questions given all
   modalities but fail to measure whether models actually use all modalities to resolve ambiguity.
   TRIAD fills this gap with 327 hand-crafted queries in 86 scene groups covering 18 ambiguity
   categories across text, image, and audio. Each item is designed so the full text–image–audio
   triplet yields a unique answer, but dropping any single modality leaves more than one consistent
   option. Evaluating 14 state-of-the-art omni-modal systems reveals that strong item-level
   accuracy does not translate to scene-level disambiguation: the best model trails humans by over
   20 points in group accuracy. Removing answer options drives model group accuracy to single
   digits, and models systematically underuse audio cues when text and image are available.

2. Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning Using Routed Ensembles and Trajectory Attribution
   Authors: Zhenshuo Zhang, Ruoxuan Xiong, Hongyang R. Zhang
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.35733 — NeurIPS 2026
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35733
   Summary: Multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL) requires a single policy to satisfy
   multiple preference-weighted reward objectives simultaneously—a problem made hard by objective
   conflict. This work introduces objective-conflict coefficients to quantify how much preference
   objectives conflict and derives online regret bounds. The authors then propose routed ensemble
   policy learning, which maintains a small set of basis policies and routes each trajectory to
   the basis policy with the highest expected return under its preference weight, alternating
   between trajectory routing and PPO updates. A complementary trajectory attribution module uses
   kernel ridge regression to estimate the marginal value of each trajectory for each preference
   objective, enabling data-efficient curriculum selection. The combined method yields strong
   empirical results on multi-objective robotic control benchmarks.

3. GeoV2V: Geometry-Grounded Video Diffusion Model for Driving Scene Generation
   Authors: Yuchen Xi, Yuhan Guo, Chenwei Hou, Zixu Liu, Jin Fang, Jason Liu, Ruigang Yang
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.25245 — ECCV 2026
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25245
   Summary: Generating novel-view driving videos (e.g., simulating a lane change from a
   recorded straight-ahead clip) requires temporal consistency and geometric accuracy under large
   viewpoint shifts. GeoV2V grounds a video-to-video diffusion model (built on Wan 2.1) with
   LiDAR and depth-based geometric priors that warp reference frames to the target viewpoint
   before generation. It also introduces Para4D, a synthetic CARLA-based dataset of synchronized
   multi-lane videos, to provide explicit cross-lane transformation supervision. Trained on
   Para4D, GeoV2V transfers zero-shot to real-world Waymo and nuScenes data, achieving
   state-of-the-art visual quality, geometric consistency, and robustness to complex lighting
   dynamics under large trajectory shifts.

4. Rethinking the Design Space of Vanilla Pixel Diffusion Transformers
   Authors: Soumik Mukhopadhyay, Long Mai, Abhinav Shrivastava, Aniruddha Mahapatra
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.23854 — under review
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23854
   Summary: Pixel-space diffusion transformers sidestep the tokenization overhead of latent
   diffusion but have lagged behind in quality, partly because choosing between x0-prediction
   and velocity prediction (u-prediction) depends non-trivially on model capacity and data
   statistics. This paper proposes MFlow, an adaptive parameterization that learns a per-
   principal-direction gain m_i interpolating between clean-image (x0) and velocity (u)
   prediction end-to-end with diffusion training. The authors show that the preferred
   parameterization shifts systematically with model capacity toward velocity prediction and
   that MFlow outperforms both fixed-parameterization baselines across ImageNet, UCF-101
   video, and large-scale text-to-image generation. The paper also identifies a persistent
   high-frequency deficit in vanilla pixel DiTs and introduces a lightweight convolutional
   module (ConvRefiner) trained post-hoc with an adversarial loss to address it.

5. TEMPO: Efficient Test-Time Adaptation For Robot Policies
   Authors: Motasem Alfarra, Pietro Mazzaglia, Markus Peschl, Daniel Dijkman, Christos Louizos
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.20318 — NeurIPS 2026
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20318
   Summary: Reinforcement-learned locomotion policies trained in simulation degrade when
   deployed conditions differ—payloads change, terrain shifts, sensors drift—yet the
   observations gathered during deployment contain the information needed to adapt. TEMPO
   addresses this with an entropy regularizer on the critic that keeps the policy from
   collapsing under distribution shift, paired with a score-based memory that upweights
   hard observations during adaptation. Evaluated on Go1, Go2, and G1 quadrupeds in
   MuJoCo Playground under four representative distribution shifts, TEMPO yields consistent
   gains and up to 80% performance improvement in a single-device setting. On a physical
   Unitree Go2 carrying an added payload, on-device adaptation with TEMPO recovers the
   pre-shift performance baseline.
