1. Rectified Language Flow
   Authors: Baiyu Su, Runlong Liao, Lizhang Chen, Qiang Liu
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.33811 — cs.AI / cs.LG (UT Austin)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33811
   Summary: Autoregressive language models generate tokens one at a time, while discrete
   diffusion models suffer from slow iterative denoising. Rectified Language Flow (ReLF)
   bypasses both limitations by training a flow model that moves continuously from noise
   to token embeddings, with a rectified (straight-path) objective that supports few-step
   generation. Fixed token embeddings are constructed via Hellinger-SVD, self-conditioning
   guidance is trained with cross-entropy, and a regularized reflow stage enables 1–4-step
   sampling without quality collapse. A novel "singular logits" mechanism handles the
   final discrete commitment as the flow approaches the data manifold. ReLF achieves lower
   generative perplexity than autoregressive baselines on OpenWebText at matched diversity,
   and outperforms prior continuous flow methods such as ELF across all evaluated model
   sizes, while generating sequences 15–19× faster at batch size 1.

2. MATCH: Execution-Aware Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning for Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments
   Authors: Shuhao Ye, Yufei Wei, Demao Wang, Shichao Zhai, Rong Xiong, Yue Wang
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.38248 — cs.RO (submitted 28 Oct 2026)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38248
   Summary: Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE) requires
   an embodied agent to follow free-form natural language instructions in realistic 3D
   scenes with continuous action spaces. Prior hierarchical methods optimize the high-level
   planner separately from a fixed low-level executor, creating a mismatch between planned
   subgoals and what the executor can actually achieve. MATCH jointly learns a
   language-conditioned planner that produces intermediate waypoints and a goal-conditioned
   executor that produces primitive actions, training both end-to-end with RL. Execution
   feedback is propagated back to inform waypoint selection, closing the loop between
   planning and control. With only 0.5B parameters, MATCH achieves state-of-the-art
   performance on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE benchmarks, surpassing prior methods by a
   meaningful margin on success rate and goal-progress metrics.

3. Can LLMs Uncover Hidden Physical Laws through Free Exploration?
   Authors: Zhen Yang, Wen Wang, Xander Xu, Ying-Cong Chen
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.37649 — cs.CL; NeurIPS 2026 ML4PS Workshop
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37649
   Summary: Can a language model act as a scientist—designing experiments, interpreting
   results, and formulating laws—without being told what to look for? LongChang provides
   an automated framework for this question by constructing MuJoCo simulation worlds in
   which one or more physical constants are silently altered. Twelve frontier LLMs are
   deployed as free explorers that design their own queries, build their own tools, and
   must ultimately formulate the hidden law. Among 12 evaluated models, GPT-6-Astra
   achieves a discovery rate of 16.7% while all others score 0%, suggesting that even
   the most capable current models largely fail at open-ended physical discovery. Analysis
   of exploration records reveals that models frequently notice unexplained anomalies but
   fail to systematically investigate them, pointing to a gap between anomaly detection
   and hypothesis formation in LLMs.

4. Vision-Language Agents Can Learn in Their Own Latent Worlds
   Authors: Haoxi Li, Sikai Bai, Jianfei Ma, Qinglin Hou, Xiaocheng Lu, Tao Han, Jie Zhang, Song Guo
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.36925 — cs.AI (submitted 27 Oct 2026)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36925
   Summary: Model-based reinforcement learning for vision-language agents typically
   requires reconstructing future frames, which is computationally expensive and may
   focus the world model on perceptually salient but task-irrelevant details. Dwell
   introduces a different approach: it treats a vision-language model as a recurrent
   latent transition operator that predicts the next latent state directly, without
   pixel-level reconstruction. Latent states are grounded using a frozen V-JEPA2 visual
   encoder, and the VLM itself learns to imagine task-relevant transitions in that space.
   Evaluated across five interactive visual environments, Dwell achieves the strongest
   overall performance among trainable methods while requiring substantially less compute
   for imagination than reconstruction-based baselines. The approach suggests that useful
   world models for VLM agents need not be pixel-faithful, but must capture the aspects
   of the environment that matter for acting.

5. Collective Emergent Alignment: Measuring and Amplifying Interaction-Induced Reasoning Alignment in Multi-Agent LLMs
   Authors: Boxuan Wang, Tianxiang Sun, Linyang Li, Xipeng Qiu, Hang Yan
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.34836 — cs.AI (submitted 26 Oct 2026)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34836
   Summary: When multiple LLMs collaborate on a reasoning task, they often converge
   toward more reference-grounded answers than any individual agent would reach alone.
   This paper asks whether this collective gain is real and separable from matched
   single-agent self-refinement. The authors define Collective Emergent Alignment (CEA)
   as the reference-grounded reasoning improvement that arises specifically from
   inter-agent interaction. They introduce REMAP (Reference-Entailment-Matrix for
   Agentic Process), which quantifies this effect through Semantic Coverage and
   interaction-specific alignment gains. Experiments across seven model families and
   two multi-hop reasoning benchmarks show that CEA exists, varies systematically with
   communication style, and can be amplified by replacing full reasoning-trace exchange
   with compact, gap-aware, selectively routed factual messages. The framework provides
   both a measurement tool for diagnosing multi-agent system quality and a practical
   method for improving it.
