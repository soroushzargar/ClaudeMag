1. General Quantification of Covariate and Concept Shifts [ICML 2026]
   Authors: Hongbo Chen, Li Charlie Xia
   Affiliation: Department of Statistics and Financial Mathematics, School of Mathematics,
                South China University of Technology, Guangzhou, China
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.11918 — Accepted, ICML 2026
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11918
   Summary: Generalization under distribution shift is a core challenge in modern machine
   learning, yet existing learning bound theory is restricted to narrow, idealized settings
   and is non-estimable from samples. This paper shows that existing definitions of concept
   shift break when source and target supports mismatch. Leveraging entropic optimal
   transport, the authors propose gamma*-concept shifts and derive a general error bound
   unifying covariate and gamma*-concept shifts that applies to broad loss functions, label
   spaces, and stochastic labeling. They further develop estimators with concentration
   guarantees and the DataShifts algorithm, which can quantify distribution shifts and
   estimate the error bound in most practical applications.

2. MindTopo: Can Foundation Models Reason in Topological Space? [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Yunfei Ge, Anbang Liu, Qineng Wang, Johnalbert Garnica, Jianwen Lyu,
            Zihan Wang, Reuben Tan, Jianfeng Gao, Ruohan Zhang, Yining Hong,
            Jiajun Wu, Manling Li
   Affiliation: Northwestern University; Microsoft Research; Stanford University;
                and collaborating institutions
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.11900
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11900
   Summary: Spatial reasoning depends not only on metric properties such as distance,
   angle, and shape, but also on topological relations that remain invariant under
   continuous deformation. Cognitive science identifies these relations as foundational
   to spatial understanding, yet foundation-model evaluations largely focus on metric or
   viewpoint-dependent relations. MindTopo introduces a benchmark of topological intuition
   across five properties grounded in cognitive science and formal topology: continuity,
   separation, order, enclosure, and knots. The benchmark contains 11,030 instances across
   13 procedurally generated task types at two cognitive levels — reasoning and planning.
   Testing 14 multimodal LLMs and 3 video generative models, the best model scores only
   61.4% against 97.9% human performance.

3. Caption-once, Frames-on-Demand: Visual-Need Routing for Budget-Aware Agentic Long
   Video Understanding [EMNLP 2026]
   Authors: Weitong Cai, Hang Zhang, Yukai Huang, Yiqiao Xie, Shan Gao,
            Jiankang Deng, Songcen Xu, Jifei Song, Zhensong Zhang
   Affiliation: Imperial College London; Huawei Noah's Ark Lab; and collaborating institutions
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.11899 — Accepted, EMNLP 2026 Main Conference
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11899
   Summary: Long-video understanding on edge devices must reason over hours of content
   under tight compute and bandwidth budgets. Subsampling visual tokens loses temporal
   structure while text-only memories lose fine-grained visual attributes. This paper
   identifies a visual-textual duality: language memories carry long-range temporal
   structure better than dense frames, while pixels remain decisive for attribute-level
   perception. The proposed CFD (Caption-once, Frames-on-Demand) framework runs a single
   offline captioning pass that builds a dual-track narrative index — an event-level story
   skeleton plus a clip-level micro-log — cached and reused across queries. At query time,
   a cloud-side MLLM reasons over the index via a lightweight Visual-Need Router that
   triggers bounded keyframe retrieval only for perceptual questions, keeping all others
   in language space.

4. CausalArena: Benchmarking Causal Discovery in the Foundation Model Era [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Zi-Rong Li, Si-Yang Liu, Tian-Zuo Wang, Han-Jia Ye
   Affiliation: Nanjing University
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.11897
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11897
   Summary: Causal discovery aims to uncover causal structures from data and is fundamental
   to scientific reasoning and intervention-based decision making. Its evaluation relies on
   structural causal models (SCMs), yet existing studies differ substantially in graph
   families, mechanisms, and evaluation protocols. The emergence of causal discovery
   foundation models (CDFMs) complicates evaluation further: performance may reflect
   pretraining environment overlap with test SCMs rather than genuine causal discovery
   ability, making fixed synthetic benchmarks unreliable. CausalArena provides a unified
   and evolvable benchmark under a common protocol that separately evaluates classical
   algorithms, CDFMs, and LLM-based causal agents, enabling meaningful comparison across
   paradigms and detecting memorization artifacts in foundation model evaluations.

5. OmniKVQuant: KV Cache Quantization for Omni-LLMs [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Suho Yoo, Hyunjong Ok, Jongmin Choi, Jihoo Jung, Joon Son Chung
   Affiliation: KAIST; POSTECH
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.11582
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11582
   Summary: Omni-modal large language models (Omni-LLMs) that process audio, video, and
   text together incur rapidly growing KV cache memory costs. KV cache quantization is the
   de facto approach for text-only LLMs, but its application to Omni-LLMs is unexplored.
   This paper analyzes how TurboQuant, a representative rotation-based KV cache
   quantization method, behaves on multimodal caches and identifies two critical failure
   modes: temporal key drift (keys shift systematically over time in audio/video streams,
   breaking fixed-range quantization) and heterogeneous value geometry (value distributions
   vary sharply across modalities, causing per-channel rotation to misalign). OmniKVQuant
   addresses these with a training-free framework: per-window key quantization ranges and
   per-modality value rotation. On Qwen2.5-Omni and Qwen3-Omni, OmniKVQuant achieves
   2-bit KV caches while substantially preserving performance across seven audio-visual
   benchmarks, with a fused Triton decode kernel that eliminates any FP16 cache rebuild.
