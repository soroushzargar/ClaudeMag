1. Beyond Repeated Sampling: Learning Search Policies for LLM Reasoning [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Ismail Labiad, Matthieu Kowalski, Marc Schoenauer, Rémi Munos, Julia Kempe
   Affiliation: Meta FAIR
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.26704
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26704
   Summary: Naive repeated sampling for LLM test-time compute generates near-duplicate attempts
   because exploration occurs only through local decoding noise. This paper proposes semantic-level
   exploration by first sampling problem-specific concepts, hints, or strategies and conditioning
   answer generation on them. A small concept generator is optimized with reinforcement learning
   to maximize the downstream pass@k of a larger, frozen answer generator. The trained concept
   generator substantially outperforms naive repeated sampling and untuned larger models on hard
   mathematical reasoning problems, and transfers to answer generators it was never trained against,
   including models from different families.

2. Hallucination Neurons and Where to Find Them: An Investigation into the Existence of Hallucination Neurons [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Not specified
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.29781
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29781
   Summary: Sparse probing methods claim to identify small sets of neurons that causally detect
   hallucinations in LLMs, but these claims are rarely tested against known failure modes of
   L1-regularized probing in correlated, high-dimensional feature spaces. This paper proposes a
   five-step diagnostic protocol covering feature correlation, bootstrap stability, sparse vs dense
   ranking disagreement, intervention baselines, and cross-dataset evaluation. Applied to H-Neurons
   in Gemma 3 4B across TriviaQA, BioASQ, and NQ-Open, it finds 19 of 22 selected neurons have
   |r| > 0.7 with other features, moderate bootstrap stability, and weak sparse-dense ranking
   overlap, showing that sparse predictive structure coexists with non-unique neuron selection.

3. Scaling of Capability and Efficiency at Inference Time in Large Reasoning Models [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Not specified
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.27166
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27166
   Summary: Capability (solve rate) and efficiency (tokens to solution) in large reasoning models
   are characterized jointly using hierarchical Bayesian models on the DeepSeek-R1-Distill family
   across four classes of arithmetic and algorithmic problems. At fixed model size, solve probability
   decays approximately exponentially with instance size. The characteristic scale of this decay
   increases as a power law with model parameters. Among correctly solved instances, output length
   grows approximately as a power law with instance size, providing the first quantitative joint
   account of capability and efficiency scaling at inference time.

4. Body-Grounded Replanning for Physically Adaptive Manipulation [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Namiko Saito, Hiroshi Kera
   Affiliation: Microsoft Research Asia (Tokyo); Waseda University; Chiba University; National Institute of Informatics
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.30024
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30024
   Summary: A manipulation strategy may remain geometrically feasible while becoming physically
   unsuitable due to increased joint load or limited mobility. This paper proposes body-grounded
   high-level replanning, where body-state events trigger LLM-based strategy selection that
   interprets joint-level state, recent execution statistics, and execution history to choose a
   context-dependent alternative while leaving the task objective and low-level controller
   unchanged. Evaluated on a reaching task under controlled load and asymmetric mobility
   constraints, body-grounded replanning maintains high task success while reducing physical
   effort and enabling more efficient strategy adaptation.

5. On the Diffusibility of High-Dimensional Latents [ECCV 2026]
   Authors: Chao Feng, Zhiyang Xu, Bowei Chen, Yuanjun Xiong, Xiyao Wang, Jui-Hsien Wang, Richard Zhang, Zhe Lin, Andrew Owens, Yijun Li
   Affiliation: Not fully specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.28473 — Accepted, ECCV 2026
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28473
   Summary: Representation Autoencoders (RAEs) let diffusion models operate in pretrained visual
   encoder feature spaces, but reconstruction-finetuned encoders exhibit a sharp collapse in
   effective dimensionality. Standard velocity prediction in flow matching must then fit orthogonal
   noise directions outside the low-dimensional signal manifold, making optimization inefficient.
   This paper shows that switching to the clean data parameterization (x₀-prediction) focuses
   learning on the signal manifold and substantially improves generation quality in high-dimensional
   RAE latent spaces, providing an analysis grounded in the geometry of reconstruction-tuned
   representations.
