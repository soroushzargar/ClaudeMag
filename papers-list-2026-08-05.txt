1. DiffusionGemma Technical Report [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Adrien Ali Taïga et al. (DiffusionGemma Team, Google DeepMind)
   Affiliation: Google DeepMind
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.00146
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00146
   Summary: DiffusionGemma is an experimental open-weight language model that uses discrete
   diffusion to generate text at exceptionally high speed. Rather than decoding one token at
   a time, it iteratively refines blocks of 256 tokens in parallel, eliminating the sequential
   bottleneck of conventional autoregressive LLMs. The model is built by fine-tuning the
   Mixture-of-Experts Gemma 4 backbone (26B total / 3.8B active parameters) with a
   compute-efficient two-stage pipeline: supervised fine-tuning teaches bidirectional denoising,
   and a second stage combines reinforcement learning with sampler distillation to improve
   quality and efficiency jointly. DiffusionGemma achieves 1000+ tokens per second on a single
   NVIDIA H100 and 700+ on an RTX 5090—up to 4× faster than equivalent autoregressive models—
   while fitting within 18 GB VRAM when quantized, enabling consumer-GPU deployment.

2. Why Large Language Models Fail at Tabular Prediction [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Marta Garnelo, Wojciech M. Czarnecki
   Affiliation: Google DeepMind
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.02412
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02412
   Summary: LLMs are routinely outperformed by simple classical baselines on tabular prediction,
   yet the cause has remained obscure. Garnelo and Czarnecki run controlled experiments on 31
   benchmark datasets to evaluate five hypotheses for the failure: inability to handle noise,
   the CSV format obscuring structure, numeric tokenisation, query batch size, and input
   dimensionality. Experiments falsify the first four. Dimensionality is decisive: the LLM is
   the only method among nine whose accuracy monotonically falls as dimension grows, while every
   classical baseline stays flat or improves. In two dimensions the LLM's predictions match a
   local distance-based method with up to 91.6% grid agreement, but in higher dimensions no
   classical learner reproduces its behavior—suggesting the prediction mechanism itself changes
   with dimension in a qualitatively novel way.

3. CMuon: Accelerating and Stabilizing Diffusion Transformer Training via Chunked Momentum Orthogonalization [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Chuyan Chen, Peng Sun, Kun Yuan
   Affiliation: Not specified (ECCV 2026)
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.02502 / ECCV 2026
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02502
   Summary: Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) achieve state-of-the-art image synthesis but require
   enormous training compute. The Muon optimizer (Momentum Orthogonalization) promised relief,
   but its direct application to DiTs causes suboptimal late-stage convergence because standard
   DiT architectures fuse functionally distinct weight blocks (AdaLN, QKV) into single tensors.
   Applying Muon to these fused matrices couples independent subspaces, distorting update
   directions. CMuon fixes this with one simple change: partition fused matrices into independent
   chunks before orthogonalization. A 675M-parameter DiT trained with CMuon achieves FID 1.18
   on ImageNet 256×256 in just 200 epochs—more than 2× faster than AdamW—while eliminating the
   convergence plateau of vanilla Muon. Accepted to ECCV 2026.

4. TRAM: Enhancing Multimodal Reasoning with Trajectory-Derived Auxiliary Memory [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Kang Liu, Zijing Wang, Yongkang Liu, Mengjie Zhao, Xiaocui Yang, Shi Feng,
            Yifei Zhang, Daling Wang
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.01922
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01922
   Summary: Multimodal Large Reasoning Models (MLRMs) excel at vision + multi-step inference
   but degrade as reasoning chains grow long: intermediate conclusions established early in the
   trajectory lose influence by the time later steps need them. TRAM is a training-free method
   that augments standard MLRM decoding with an auxiliary memory pathway derived from the
   reasoning trajectory itself. Attribution analysis reveals that correctness is more closely
   associated with whether trajectories retain and integrate reasoning-derived information
   across stages than with visual attribution alone. By explicitly maintaining a compact
   trajectory memory and injecting it into decoding, TRAM improves accuracy on long-chain
   multimodal reasoning benchmarks without any fine-tuning.

5. Computational and Statistical Guarantees of the c-Rectified Flow [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Leda Wang, Zhehao Xu, Qiang Liu, Harrison H. Zhou
   Affiliation: UT Austin; Yale University
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.02487
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02487
   Summary: Rectified Flow and flow matching have become dominant frameworks for fast generative
   modeling, yet theoretical guarantees for learning the optimal-transport (OT) solution have
   lagged. c-Rectified Flow is a cost-aware variant that modifies the flow matching objective
   to restrict vector fields to the form ∇c̄(∇f_t(·)), where c̄ is the convex conjugate of the
   transport cost c. Wang et al. provide both computational guarantees (iterative application
   monotonically reduces c-transport cost while preserving marginals) and statistical guarantees
   (sample complexity bounds for the learned flow from finite data). They also delineate when
   the method recovers the true OT solution and when strong assumptions such as connected support
   or smoothness are required, providing a rigorous foundation for cost-aware generative modeling.
