1. CompKV: Compensation-Aware KV Selection for Long-Context LLM Inference [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Anonymous et al.
   Affiliation: Not publicly available
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.26300
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26300
   Summary: Existing sparse-attention KV cache methods select blocks based on attention mass alone
   and discard the unselected context outright. CompKV derives a joint compensation-aware selection
   criterion by pairing block selection with mean compensation—replacing every logit in an unselected
   block with its block mean—which admits an exact log-partition identity. Analysis shows that the
   optimal selection priority depends jointly on a block's attention mass and the within-block logit
   variation. An efficient GPU implementation keeps the complete BF16 KV cache and FP32 block
   summaries in pinned CPU memory with a fused kernel architecture. Evaluated on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct,
   Qwen3-8B, and Qwen3-32B over RULER and LongBench-Pro, CompKV consistently outperforms prior
   selection-only and eviction methods under tight exact-read budgets.

2. Flash-dLLM: IO-Aware KV Caching and Parallel Decoding for Fast, Memory-Efficient Diffusion LLMs [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Quan Nguyen-Tri, Mukul Ranjan, Zhiqiang Shen
   Affiliation: VILA Lab, Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence (MBZUAI)
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.26796
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26796
   Summary: Diffusion language models (dLLMs) offer non-autoregressive generation but their inference
   is bottlenecked by GPU memory I/O rather than compute when KV caching is naively applied. Flash-dLLM
   identifies this IO bottleneck and addresses it with an IO-aware fused KV-cache kernel that eliminates
   redundant memory movement. It further proposes a KV-cache-driven draft-and-verify decoding strategy
   in which the dLLM itself serves as both drafter and verifier, eliminating the need for an auxiliary
   model. On mathematical reasoning (GSM8K) and code generation (HumanEval), Flash-dLLM achieves 5.1×
   and 11.0× speedups respectively over the Elastic-Cache baseline while improving memory efficiency.

3. Recursive self-improvement of AI research agents [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Dhruv Srikanth, Bingchen Zhao, Dixing Xu, Yuxiang Wu, Zhengyao Jiang
   Affiliation: Weco AI
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.26457
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26457
   Summary: This paper introduces AIDE² (AI Development and Execution squared), a bi-level system
   for recursive self-improvement of an AI research agent. An outer agent rewrites the inner AIDE
   agent's own code—its search policy, prompts, and memory mechanisms—evaluates each proposed
   modification against a diverse battery of AI R&D tasks, and retains only changes that improve
   held-out performance. In an unattended 8-day run, AIDE² discovered seven successive improvements
   including a novel search algorithm and 16× prompt compression. The evolved agent reduced its
   reward-hacking rate from 63% to 34% on a held-out benchmark and matched or exceeded a
   human-engineered production research agent on four out-of-distribution held-out benchmarks
   spanning ML engineering, heuristic algorithm engineering, and physics-based weather forecasting.

4. From Token Importance to Conditional Removability: Rethinking Visual Token Pruning in Multimodal Large Language Models [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Shengli He et al.
   Affiliation: Not fully specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.26484
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26484
   Summary: Training-free visual token pruning in multimodal LLMs typically scores tokens by importance
   or redundancy and removes low-scoring ones, but these criteria do not fully capture removability,
   which is conditioned on representation depth and the surrounding deletion set. Controlled
   interventions show that removing the same tokens at different transformer layers produces
   substantially different downstream perturbations, and that changing only the deletion context
   alters pruning-boundary decisions. The paper proposes CoRePrune, a two-stage training-free
   framework: Progressive Perturbation-Aware Visual Pruning refreshes deletion-effect estimates as
   representations evolve through depth, while Set-Conditioned Refinement re-evaluates candidates
   under the current deletion set after visual-text interaction. Across five MLLM backbones covering
   standard images, high-resolution inputs, and video, CoRePrune retains 90.3% of dense-model
   performance with 128 visual tokens while reducing aggregate prefill time by 51.0%.

5. Greedy Decoding Is Not Precision-Invariant: Cross-Precision Output Divergence in LLM Inference [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Gaoyuan Du, Anam Nawaz Khan, Rex Zhou, Xiaoyang Liu, Deepayan Chakrabarti, Fnu Suya, Xueping Li
   Affiliation: University of Tennessee Knoxville; University of Chicago; Amazon; University of Texas at Austin
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.26621
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26621
   Summary: Greedy decoding is commonly assumed to be deterministic given a fixed model and prompt,
   but this paper demonstrates that BF16 and FP16 produce different outputs on identical hardware
   for the same model. Across six models (1.1B–7B parameters, four families) and three benchmarks,
   49–100% of prompts diverge, with a single token flip often cascading into full trajectory-level
   divergence. Empirical error-propagation analysis shows that 22 layers of accumulated body error
   do not predict divergence; rather, the outcome depends on the top-two logit margin at the language
   model head relative to the directional perturbation between the top-two candidates. Selective FP32
   lm-head recomputation, triggered only when the margin falls below a threshold, achieves +22–36
   percentage-point exact agreement at only ±4% latency overhead on A10G.
