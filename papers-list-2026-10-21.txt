1. Learning to Steer LLM Agents and Multi-Agent Systems
   Authors: Leyang Shen, Yaxin Gong, et al.
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.22316
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22316
   Summary: LLM agents and multi-agent systems suffer from goal drift—execution deviates from the
   overall objective as steps accumulate, wasting resources and causing undesired outcomes. The
   authors propose a dedicated steering agent that monitors ongoing execution and intervenes via
   two paths: a low-cost fast path for common corrective actions and a high-cost slow path for
   complex interventions. Both are jointly trained with GRPO using a pairwise ranking objective.
   Experiments on information-seeking tasks show the steering agent improves performance and
   efficiency for both single-agent and multi-agent settings, and the learned policy transfers to
   out-of-domain tasks and unseen executors without retraining.

2. PACE: Pareto-Adaptive Compression for Efficient Native MLLMs
   Authors: Lei Tian, Chunzheng Zhu, Kai Wu, Yifan Zhang, Haihua Yang
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.24005 — NeurIPS 2026
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24005
   Summary: Native multimodal large language models (MLLMs) process images directly without a
   separate vision encoder, creating new challenges for visual token compression that prior
   ViT-based methods cannot address. PACE decomposes spatial attention into orthogonal height and
   width saliency estimates gated by a positional attention signal, and treats token budget
   allocation as a Pareto optimization over performance preservation, inference efficiency, and
   spatial coverage. At only 25–50% visual token retention, PACE maintains near-lossless
   performance and even improves on several benchmarks, while delivering over 2× FLOPs reduction
   against uncompressed baselines. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026.

3. NestedVLA: Learning to Consolidate and Generate Skills for Vision-Language-Action Models
   Authors: Guodong Du, Wanyu Lin
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.23271 — NeurIPS 2026
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23271
   Summary: Consolidating manipulation skills across heterogeneous tasks is hindered by cross-
   platform data splits, multi-task training interference, and parameter conflicts in model
   merging. NestedVLA sidesteps these issues by learning skills independently first and
   consolidating post hoc through generation rather than aggregation. A nested hypernetwork,
   conditioned on the target task, synthesizes task-adaptive action-expert parameter deltas that
   compose with a frozen base VLA. Task-specific VLM residuals are stored as compressed patches
   enabling flexible reuse. On MetaWorld, ManiSkill, and CALVIN, NestedVLA outperforms the
   strongest model-merging baseline by 41 pp on average. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026.

4. Intra-Rubric Reward Divergence: Mitigating Rubric Hacking through Latent Dimension
   Recovery and Balancing
   Authors: Jianqiang Xia, Han Xu, Dianxi Shi
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.26685
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26685
   Summary: In rubric-based reinforcement learning for language models, total rubric score can
   rise while individual sub-dimension scores systematically fall—a phenomenon the authors call
   Intra-Rubric Reward Divergence (IRRD), driven by dimension imbalance and dimension absence in
   rubric specification. They introduce two remedies: Fair-Race Dimension Aggregation (FRDA),
   which has generations compete within each sub-dimension separately, and Residual Semantic
   Dimension Completion (RSDC), which recovers evaluation dimensions latent in holistic preference
   signals to fill rubric gaps. Across chat, science, and medical evaluation domains, FRDA yields
   more stable anti-hacking improvements and RSDC outperforms both single-reward training and
   naive multi-reward aggregation.

5. Agent World Model in the Wild: Computer-Use Agent Environment Synthesis
   Authors: Zhaoyang Wang, Qianhui Wu, Huaxiu Yao, et al.
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.26828
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26828
   Summary: Training general computer-use agents requires realistic, controllable environments
   that remain scarce and expensive to build manually. WildAWM synthesizes such environments by
   grounding them in user personas and daily activities: it generates executable personal-computer
   worlds with websites, MCP services, and local files, then synthesizes cross-agent interface
   (XAI) tasks with execution-based quality gates combining heterogeneous agent rollouts,
   cross-judge consistency checks, and counterfactual probes of judgment robustness. The resulting
   341 long-horizon task-environment pairs require over 100 agent steps to solve; GPT-6-Astra
   solves only 60% of them. Qwen models trained exclusively in WildAWM environments achieve
   substantial gains across 8 out-of-distribution benchmarks spanning web, desktop, and
   computer-use tasks.
