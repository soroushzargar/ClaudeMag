1. Explorative Modeling: Unlocking a Third Pretraining Axis and End-to-End Generation [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Alexi Gladstone, Heng Ji, Yilun Du
   Affiliation: University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign; MIT
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2607.27372
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27372
   Summary: Standard generative model training minimizes the expected loss over all data points, which
   causes predictions to "blur" across modes of the data distribution rather than committing to any
   one. Explorative Modeling (XM) factors the training loop: at each step, K candidate generations
   are drawn and only the one that best matches the target is used for the gradient update. This
   introduces exploration as a third pretraining axis beyond parameters and data. Increasing K
   monotonically improves performance across images, video, and language, with Explorative Models
   reaching 6.2× sample efficiency, 4.1× FLOP efficiency, and 47% better parameter efficiency
   compared to standard training. XM achieves a near-SOTA FID of 1.43 on ImageNet without guidance.

2. TurboVLA: Real-Time Vision-Language-Action Model at 32 Hz on an RTX 4090 with <1 GB VRAM [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Hengyi Xie, Chenfei Yao, Xianjin Wu, Xuanyang Xi, Yiping Tang, Di Xu,
            Yingying Zhu, Dingkang Liang, Xiang Bai, Han Ding
   Affiliation: Huazhong University of Science and Technology; Huawei Technologies Co. Ltd
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2607.27205
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27205
   Summary: Vision-language-action (VLA) models for robotics commonly adopt a V→L→A pathway in
   which visual observations are projected into a large language model's representation space before
   being decoded into actions, incurring substantial computation and memory overhead at every policy
   invocation. TurboVLA replaces this with a direct V+L→A mapping: visual observations and language
   instructions are independently encoded, then combined via lightweight bidirectional vision-language
   interaction, and continuous action chunks are predicted with a compact decoder. The result runs at
   32 Hz (31.2 ms per step) on an RTX 4090, uses only 0.9 GB VRAM at inference, and achieves 97.7%
   average success on LIBERO with only 0.2B parameters—matching or outperforming substantially larger
   LLM-centric policies.

3. RLPF: Reinforcement Learning from Performance Feedback for Code Generation [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Huihao Jing, Haozhe Cui, Wenbin Hu, Shaojin Chen, Haochen Shi, Changxuan Fan,
            Yuxuan Liu, Hanyu Yang, Sirui Zhang, Ziyi Chen, Haoran Li, Yangqiu Song
   Affiliation: Hong Kong University of Science and Technology
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2607.27271
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27271
   Summary: Code models are increasingly trained with execution feedback, but most training signals
   stop at correctness. RLPF studies how to train code agents to prefer faster correct
   implementations, treating efficiency as a first-class training objective rather than an evaluation
   metric. The key challenge is that runtime is a fragile reward—it is meaningful only after a
   program is correct, varies across tasks, and gives little gradient signal when most sampled
   programs fail to compile. RLPF introduces a staged reward: a correctness stage ensures programs
   are runnable before a performance stage rewards relative speedup. On a family-disjoint test split,
   RLPF raises a weak base model from 11.1% to 54.6% correct-and-runnable rate and from 8.1% to
   38.6% CGRE (Correct-and-Graded Relative Efficiency).

4. MemSFT: Mitigating Alignment Tax with an External Parametric Memory [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Jiarui Wang, Xiang Shi, Jiaqi Cao, Rubin Wei, Xiquan Wang, Hao Sun,
            Jingzhi Wang, Zhiqi Yang, Qipeng Guo, Bowen Zhou, Zhouhan Lin
   Affiliation: Shanghai Jiao Tong University; Fudan University; Tencent AI Lab
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2607.25614
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25614
   Summary: Fine-tuning LLMs on domain-specific data improves performance in that domain but
   degrades general capabilities—a phenomenon termed the alignment tax. MemSFT avoids this by
   decoupling domain specialization from backbone parameter updates: a compact plug-and-play
   parametric memory module is trained to imitate a non-parametric retriever over domain data,
   memorizing domain-specific knowledge without touching the backbone. During generation, a learned
   router dynamically fuses the output distributions of the memory and backbone at each decoding
   step. Once trained for a domain, the memory can be reused across LLMs of different sizes.
   Experiments across biology, geoscience, and law with models from Qwen3-8B to Qwen3-235B-A22B
   show consistent domain improvement with negligible degradation on general tasks, whereas full SFT
   causes severe catastrophic forgetting.

5. SkillRise: Agentic Reinforcement Learning for Cross-Task Skill Evolution [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Zhiyuan Yao, Yuxin Chen, Zhengxi Lu, Zishan Xu, Yueqing Sun, Yifu Guo, Yuquan Lu,
            Zhengzhou Cai, Kangning Zhang, Zhuowen Han, Zihan Wang, Ziang Ye, Qi Gu,
            Xunliang Cai, Weiwen Liu, Yongliang Shen
   Affiliation: Zhejiang University; National University of Singapore; Shanghai Jiao Tong University;
                Meituan
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2607.26784
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26784
   Summary: Standard agentic reinforcement learning treats each task as an independent episode,
   missing the opportunity to transfer learned workflows to future tasks. SkillRise trains a single
   policy that simultaneously solves tasks and curates an evolving skill document: given a task, the
   agent attempts to solve it while updating a skill document passed as context to the next task.
   Decoupled credit assignment rewards solving with the current task outcome and skill curation with
   discounted downstream outcomes, ensuring skills are only reinforced when they demonstrably help
   future tasks. SkillRise achieves the best Pass@1 among compared methods on ALFWorld, WebShop, and
   ScienceWorld across zero-shot and few-shot conditions.
