# Concertina: Embodying Manipulation and Cognition in One Diffusion Vision-Language Model

**Authors:** Chengcheng Wang, Wei He, Wenhao Li, Hongguang Li, Kai Han, Jianyuan Guo, Chang Xu
**arXiv:** 2611.07513 (cs.CV, cs.AI, cs.RO)
**Submitted:** 5 November 2026
**Venue:** Preprint

---

## Headline

By inserting continuous action representations directly into a diffusion vision-language model backbone—with no special tokens, adapters, or soft prompts—Concertina achieves 98.15% average success on LIBERO and 85.9% on the 10,030-task LIBERO-Plus benchmark, unifying manipulation and reasoning in a single model.

---

## Key Findings

- **Concertina** unifies robotic manipulation and vision-language understanding in a single diffusion VLM backbone, with continuous action outputs generated by the same denoising process as language tokens.
- The architecture adds no action-boundary special tokens, embodiment-specific adapters, or soft prompts: actions are native to the backbone.
- Achieves **98.15% average success on LIBERO**, **85.9% on LIBERO-Plus** (10,030 tasks), and **4.142 average completed chain length on CALVIN ABC-D**.
- Code and weights are available at github.com/lose4578/Concertina and huggingface.co/Traly/concertina.

---

## Motivation and Problem

Most vision-language-action models combine a pre-trained VLM with a separately trained action module: the VLM encodes the scene and the task description, and the action module maps the encoding to motor commands. This design creates an interface problem: the VLM was not trained to produce representations that are optimal for action generation, and the action module was not trained with awareness of the VLM's reasoning process.

The ideal is a model where language understanding, visual reasoning, and action generation all happen in the same representational substrate—where the model "thinks" about what to do and "does" it in one forward pass. Diffusion VLMs, which already generate continuous outputs through iterative denoising, offer a natural path toward this ideal.

The question Concertina addresses: can continuous action outputs be integrated into a diffusion VLM backbone so naturally that no architectural additions are needed?

## Method: Concertina

### Native Action Integration

Concertina represents robotic actions as continuous-valued tokens that occupy the same token sequence as language tokens. The key insight is that a diffusion VLM already learns to denoise continuous representations during generation: by tokenizing action trajectories into the same space, action generation becomes a first-class operation for the backbone.

Formally, an action trajectory $a_{1:H} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times d_a}$ is mapped to a sequence of continuous tokens $e_{1:H}$ via a lightweight projection:

$$e_t = W_a \cdot a_t$$

These tokens are appended to the token sequence, and the diffusion backbone generates them by the same denoising objective used for text:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{action}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, \epsilon}\bigl[\|e_{1:H} - \hat{e}_{1:H}(e_{1:H}^{(t)}, z_{\text{context}})\|^2\bigr]$$

where $z_{\text{context}}$ is the backbone's contextualized encoding of the visual observation and task description.

### No Auxiliary Components

Unlike prior methods that add:
- Action-boundary special tokens (e.g., `[ACT]`, `[/ACT]`)
- Embodiment-specific adapters (LoRA modules or separate MLP heads per robot)
- Soft prompts for task conditioning

Concertina uses none of these. The backbone learns to generate actions as naturally as it generates text, with no seam between the two modalities. The projection $W_a$ is the only additional parameter.

### Training

Training is joint: the backbone is trained simultaneously on (1) standard VLM objectives (image captioning, visual QA) and (2) action generation objectives on robot demonstration data. The joint training ensures that the backbone's representations are shared between reasoning and action tasks rather than optimized for one and adapted for the other.

## Experimental Setup and Results

Benchmarks: LIBERO (50 tabletop manipulation tasks), LIBERO-Plus (10,030-task extension), CALVIN ABC-D (long-horizon object manipulation with language conditioning).

**LIBERO average success:**
| Method | LIBERO | LIBERO-Plus |
|---|---|---|
| OpenVLA | 73.1% | 42.3% |
| RoboVLMs | 81.4% | 56.2% |
| $\pi_0$ | 90.3% | 72.1% |
| Concertina (ours) | **98.15%** | **85.9%** |

**CALVIN ABC-D chain length (↑ is better):**
| Method | Avg chain length |
|---|---|
| SuSIE | 2.69 |
| $\pi_0$ | 3.41 |
| Concertina (ours) | **4.142** |

## Significance

Concertina's main contribution is architectural simplicity: by showing that a diffusion backbone can generate continuous actions natively without any structural additions, it eliminates the design complexity of VLA models and potentially improves generalization by ensuring that the same backbone representations serve both understanding and acting. The 85.9% result on 10,030-task LIBERO-Plus is a particularly strong generalization result, since LIBERO-Plus contains tasks not seen during training.
