# CLIFT: Conformal Self-Verification for Web Agent Training and Test-Time Scaling

**arXiv:** 2610.06829  
**Authors:** Yifan Zhang, Yutong Dai, Viraj Prabhu, Zhiyuan Hu, Ran Xu, Zeyuan Chen  
**Affiliation:** Georgia Institute of Technology; University of Texas at Austin  
**Date:** October 5, 2026

---

## One-Line Summary

CLIFT replaces costly frontier-LM supervision with a conformally certified URL-aware self-verifier that provides dense per-step training signals and enables reliable test-time scaling without external oracles.

---

## Problem

Training web agents with reinforcement learning faces a fundamental supervision problem. Binary task success—did the agent complete the task or not?—is the most reliable signal but provides sparse credit assignment: an agent that takes 20 steps to complete a task receives the same positive reward regardless of which intermediate steps were actually useful. Frontier language model judges can provide denser step-level feedback but are too expensive to query at every step during training and cannot be assumed to be available at deployment.

---

## Why Existing Approaches Fall Short

- **Binary rewards are sparse:** Task-level success/failure provides no information about which intermediate actions contributed to the outcome, making exploration and gradient estimation difficult.
- **LM judges are expensive:** Querying a frontier model (e.g., GPT-4o) at every step of a training episode dramatically increases cost and introduces dependencies on proprietary APIs that may not be available at deployment time.
- **Heuristic verifiers lack calibration:** Rule-based verifiers (e.g., checking URLs or DOM state against expected patterns) can fire incorrectly; without calibration, their signals may mislead training.
- **Test-time scaling is uncertified:** Existing test-time scaling methods for agents (best-of-N, chain-of-thought) provide no statistical guarantee that more compute actually improves reliability.

---

## Core Method

**CLIFT (Conformal Learning for Instruction Following at Test time)** introduces a two-part framework:

1. **Conformal Self-Verification (training):** Natural-language verification questions (e.g., "Is the agent on the correct URL?", "Has the required form field been filled?") are generated from task descriptions and grounded in the current browser state (URL + DOM). A small verifier model answers these questions and assigns a per-step score. Conformal prediction calibrates the verifier's confidence, ensuring that the verifier fires only when it can certify its answer at a pre-specified error rate. This turns the verifier into a **conformally certified URL-aware verifier** that provides non-negative, calibrated per-step evidence.

2. **Conformal Test-time Scaling (CTS, inference):** The same frozen verifier bank is used at test time to score candidate action sequences generated by the agent. CTS selects among multiple sampled trajectories based on conformal verifier scores, enabling reliable test-time scaling without querying an external oracle.

---

## Technical Formulation

Let $s_t = (u_t, d_t)$ denote the browser state at step $t$ (URL and DOM excerpt), $a_t$ the agent's action, and $Q = \{q_1, \ldots, q_K\}$ a set of verification questions derived from the task.

The verifier model $v_\phi$ scores each question-state pair:
$$p_{k,t} = v_\phi(q_k \mid s_t, a_t) \in [0,1]$$

Conformal calibration with a coverage guarantee $1-\alpha$ produces a threshold $\tau$ such that:
$$\Pr[\text{verifier incorrect}] \leq \alpha$$

The per-step evidence signal used in training is:
$$r_t = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathbf{1}[p_{k,t} \geq \tau] \cdot w_k$$

where $w_k$ are task-specific weights. This evidence is always non-negative (it is evidence that progress was made, never a penalty), so it supplements rather than replaces the sparse task-completion reward.

For CTS at test time, given $N$ sampled trajectories $\{\tau^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^N$, the trajectory with highest cumulative conformal evidence is selected:
$$\hat{\tau} = \arg\max_i \sum_t r_t^{(i)}$$

---

## Learning or Inference Procedure

**Training:**
1. Collect task descriptions and generate a bank of verification questions per task type.
2. Calibrate the verifier on a held-out set with conformal prediction to determine threshold $\tau$ at desired coverage $1-\alpha$.
3. Run RL training with the sparse task-completion reward augmented by the conformal per-step evidence $r_t$.

**Test-time scaling:**
1. Sample $N$ trajectories from the policy.
2. Score each trajectory step-by-step with the frozen conformal verifier.
3. Return the trajectory with the highest cumulative evidence score.

---

## What the Guarantee Says

By construction of conformal prediction, the verifier provides a marginal coverage guarantee: across all verification questions, the probability that the verifier incorrectly certifies an incorrect action as correct is at most $\alpha$ (typically $\alpha = 0.1$). This guarantees that per-step signals used during training are non-negative in expectation, preserving policy improvement. The CTS selection guarantee is weaker—it depends on the quality of the trajectory scoring function—but the conformal calibration ensures that the selected trajectory is at least as likely to be correct as random selection would predict.

---

## Experimental Findings

- **Training with CLIFT signals:** Compared to RL with only binary reward, CLIFT-augmented training achieves significantly higher task success rates on WebArena and VisualWebArena benchmarks.
- **Conformal evidence density:** CLIFT fires at roughly 40–60% of steps across tasks, providing substantially denser credit assignment than binary rewards.
- **CTS efficiency:** Test-time scaling with $N=8$ trajectories and CTS selection improves success rate by 8–12 percentage points over single-trajectory evaluation, without querying a frontier model.
- **Calibration quality:** The conformally calibrated verifier achieves coverage close to the nominal $1-\alpha$ level across all task types.

---

## Ablations and Interpretation

**Conformal calibration vs.\ uncalibrated verifier:** Using the raw verifier without conformal calibration leads to noisy per-step signals and can harm training; calibration is critical.

**Question quality:** Using generic verification questions rather than task-specific ones degrades performance; the quality of the question bank determines the coverage of the verifier.

**Number of CTS samples:** Performance improves with more trajectories up to $N=16$; beyond that, gains are marginal, suggesting the conformal verifier reaches its discrimination ceiling.

**Verifier model size:** A 1B parameter verifier provides comparable quality to a 7B verifier, indicating that small models suffice for structured URL-aware verification.
