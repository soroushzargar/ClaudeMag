# Thinking with Visual Primitives

**Authors:** Ruijie Lu, Yiyang Ma, Gang Zeng, Ting Chen, et al. (DeepSeek-AI; Peking University; Tsinghua University)
**arXiv:** Technical report / NeurIPS 2026 poster
**Released:** 30 April 2026; NeurIPS presentation November 2026
**Venue:** NeurIPS 2026 (poster)

---

## Headline

Interleaving spatial markers — points and bounding boxes — directly into a multimodal model's reasoning chain, treating them as minimal units of thought rather than post-hoc annotations, achieves 98.7% accuracy on spatial reasoning tasks and closes the Reference Gap that language-only tokens cannot bridge.

---

## Key Findings

- **Visual primitives** (points and bounding boxes) are proposed as the minimal units of a model's visual reasoning, analogous to tokens in language reasoning.
- Interleaving visual primitives into the chain-of-thought enables a model to "point while it reasons," grounding abstract references to specific spatial locations without image cropping or zooming.
- Achieves **98.7%** accuracy on the authors' spatial reasoning benchmark, 1–2 percentage points above the strongest competing multimodal models.
- Built on **DeepSeek-V4-Flash**, a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) backbone, with KV-cache compression that maps every 4 visual tokens to a single entry — maintaining efficiency despite dense visual reasoning.
- Closes the **Reference Gap**: language tokens are ambiguous about precise spatial locations in high-resolution or cluttered images; visual primitives resolve this ambiguity at the reasoning step rather than requiring resolution at input encoding time.
- Limitation: the primitive-based reasoning mode is currently triggered by explicit keywords in the prompt.

---

## Motivation and Problem

Multimodal language models routinely fail on tasks that require precise spatial reference in high-resolution or cluttered visual scenes. A query like "what is to the left of the second blue object in the top row?" requires the model to resolve multiple spatial references in sequence — a task where language tokens provide insufficient grounding.

Existing approaches work around this by applying image crops or zoom patches, feeding them as separate images to the model. This has three problems:
1. **Cascading errors**: a wrong crop at step one propagates through the reasoning chain.
2. **Context fragmentation**: the spatial relationship between the crop and the original image is lost.
3. **Resolution mismatch**: determining where to crop requires spatial reasoning, creating a circular dependency.

The authors identify this as the **Reference Gap**: the mismatch between the precision of spatial references in natural language and the representational capacity of text tokens over dense visual layouts.

## Method: Thinking with Visual Primitives

### Visual Primitive Tokens

La-JEPA introduces two types of visual primitive tokens that can be generated by the model and embedded in its chain of thought:

- **Point tokens** `<point x="0.42" y="0.67"/>`: normalized coordinates in the image, generated by the model as part of its reasoning.
- **Box tokens** `<box x1="0.21" y1="0.33" x2="0.58" y2="0.71"/>`: bounding boxes specifying a spatial region the model is reasoning about.

These tokens are part of the model's vocabulary and are generated auto-regressively alongside text tokens. When the model generates a primitive token, the visual encoder extracts features from the corresponding location or region and injects them into the next-token prediction context.

### Training Procedure

Training has three phases:

1. **Supervised pretraining on primitive annotation data**: the model is trained to generate correct primitive tokens given ground-truth spatial annotations.
2. **Chain-of-thought reasoning fine-tuning**: the model is fine-tuned on spatial reasoning problems with multi-step chains of thought that interleave primitive tokens.
3. **RLVR fine-tuning**: the final model is refined with verifiable reward signals (correctness of spatial reasoning outputs) using a GRPO-style algorithm.

### KV-Cache Compression

The DeepSeek-V4-Flash backbone uses a visual KV-cache compression scheme that maps every 4 consecutive visual tokens to a single cached entry, reducing the memory cost of dense visual features by 4×. This is essential for long reasoning chains that generate many primitive tokens over high-resolution images.

## Technical Formulation

The model generates sequences of mixed text and primitive tokens:

$$y = (y_1, \ldots, y_T), \quad y_i \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{text}} \cup \mathcal{V}_{\text{prim}}$$

where $\mathcal{V}_{\text{prim}}$ contains the point and box token types. At generation step $i$, if $y_i \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{prim}}$, the visual encoder $\phi_v$ extracts a feature vector from the specified image region:

$$v_i = \phi_v(I, \text{region}(y_i))$$

This feature vector is concatenated with the standard context embedding $c_i$ and passed to the next-token predictor:

$$P(y_{i+1} \mid y_{\leq i}) = \text{softmax}\!\left(W [c_i; v_i]\right)$$

For text tokens ($y_i \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{text}}$), $v_i = \mathbf{0}$ and the standard language modeling objective applies. The model learns when to generate primitive tokens and what coordinates to generate through the supervised and RLVR training phases.

## Experimental Findings

### Spatial Reasoning Benchmark

The authors introduce an in-house benchmark of 2,400 questions requiring multi-step spatial reasoning over cluttered scenes.

| Model | Accuracy |
|---|---|
| GPT-5-V (image cropping) | 97.1% |
| Gemini 2.5 Ultra | 96.8% |
| Claude Muse-V | 96.4% |
| Thinking with Visual Primitives | **98.7%** |

### Standard Multimodal Benchmarks

Performance on MMBench, SeedBench, and MMStar (standard multimodal evaluation) is maintained or marginally improved relative to the base DeepSeek-V4-Flash model, confirming that primitive-based reasoning adds spatial capability without degrading general multimodal performance.

### Ablations

- **Without RLVR fine-tuning**: accuracy drops to 94.1%, confirming that reward-based refinement is essential.
- **With text-only reasoning** (no primitive tokens): 89.3%, establishing the baseline gap.
- **With image cropping** instead of primitives: 93.7%, showing that primitives outperform the standard workaround.

---

## Significance

Thinking with Visual Primitives represents a qualitative shift in how multimodal models handle spatial reasoning: rather than engineering around the Reference Gap with image preprocessing, it closes the gap at the reasoning architecture level. The approach is general — any spatial reference task benefits — and the primitive token design is model-agnostic. The connection to chain-of-thought reasoning suggests that visual primitives could become a standard component of multimodal reasoning systems, analogous to tool calls in language agents.
