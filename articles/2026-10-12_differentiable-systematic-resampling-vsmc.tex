{\LARGE\bfseries\raggedright
  Differentiable Systematic Resampling for Variational Sequential Monte Carlo}\par\vspace{4pt}

{\large\itshape\raggedright
  A Sigmoid Softening of the CDF Ancestor Assignment Recovers Systematic
  Resampling's Variance Advantage While Allowing Full Gradient Flow}\par\vspace{6pt}

{\small\textbf{By} Fredrik Cumlin \& Saikat Chatterjee}\par\vspace{2pt}

{\small\color{keywordgray}arXiv:2610.12094 \textbullet{} NeurIPS 2026 \textbullet{} October 2026}
\par\vspace{2pt}\noindent{\color{rulered}\rule{\columnwidth}{0.6pt}}\vspace{6pt}

Systematic resampling is the most variance-efficient particle-filter
resampling scheme, but its step-function CDF blocks gradient flow.
Differentiable Systematic Resampling (DSR) softens the CDF-level ancestor
assignment with sigmoid functions, sorts particles via a Morton space-filling
curve to preserve the banded ancestor structure, and proves that the bias
vanishes exponentially in the inverse temperature---without iterative solvers.

\section{The Big Picture}

Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) approximates temporal posterior distributions
with weighted particles $\{(w_t^k, x_t^k)\}_{k=1}^K$. Resampling---selecting
ancestors for the next particle generation---prevents degeneracy but
introduces a discontinuity that blocks gradients. Variational SMC (VSMC)
maximises an ELBO over proposal and model parameters via backpropagation
through the full particle trajectory, so differentiable resampling is
a key missing ingredient.

Systematic resampling is preferred in practice because it has $O(K^{-2})$
variance in the sample count (vs.\ $O(K^{-1})$ for multinomial) and is
$O(K)$ to compute. DSR makes it differentiable without sacrificing these
advantages.

\section{Why Existing Approaches Fall Short}

\textbf{Multinomial resampling} (Gumbel-softmax variants) is differentiable
but has higher variance; the systematic resampling advantage is lost.

\textbf{OT-based differentiable resampling} preserves the systematic
resampling's CDF-ordered structure but requires an iterative Sinkhorn solver
at every resampling step, adding substantial overhead.

\textbf{Stop-gradient resampling} avoids differentiating through resampling
but biases the ELBO gradient estimator systematically.

\section{Core Mechanism}

Standard systematic resampling draws $U \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1/K)$ and
assigns ancestor $a^j = \min\{i: W_{\leq i} \geq (j-1)/K + U\}$, where
$W_{\leq i} = \sum_{l \leq i} w^l$.

DSR replaces the step function with a sigmoid:
\[
\tilde{A}_{ij} = \sigma\!\left(\frac{W_{\leq i} - (j-1)/K - U}{\tau}\right)
- \sigma\!\left(\frac{W_{\leq i-1} - (j-1)/K - U}{\tau}\right)
\]

The soft-resampled particle is $\tilde{x}^j = \sum_i \tilde{A}_{ij}\,x^i$.

A Morton (Z-order) space-filling curve sorts particles before resampling
so that spatially proximate particles are adjacent in CDF order, preserving
the \emph{banded} ancestor matrix property that gives systematic resampling
its variance efficiency.

\section{Technical Formulation}

Let $\{w^k\}_{k=1}^K$ be normalised weights with prefix sums
$W_{\leq k} = \sum_{j \leq k} w^j$.

The soft ancestor matrix is:
\[
\tilde{A}_{ij}(\tau) = \sigma\!\left(\tfrac{W_{\leq i} - \frac{j-1}{K} - U}{\tau}\right)
- \sigma\!\left(\tfrac{W_{\leq i-1} - \frac{j-1}{K} - U}{\tau}\right)
\]

\textbf{Bias bound:} There exist constants $C, c > 0$ such that
\[
\|\mathbb{E}[\tilde{A}(\tau)] - \mathbb{E}[A]\|_\infty \leq C e^{-c/\tau}
\]

This exponential rate is proved from the pointwise approximation error
of the sigmoid to the Heaviside step function.

\section{Learning or Inference Procedure}

\textbf{Variational SMC with DSR:}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item Propagate: $x_t^k \sim q_\phi(x_t \mid x_{t-1}^k, y_t)$.
  \item Weight: $w_t^k \propto
    p_\theta(y_t | x_t^k)\,p_\theta(x_t^k | x_{t-1}^k)
    / q_\phi(x_t^k | x_{t-1}^k, y_t)$.
  \item Sort particles via Morton curve.
  \item Apply DSR to obtain $\{\tilde{x}_t^k\}$.
  \item Accumulate ELBO: $\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}[\log \frac{1}{K}\sum_k w_T^k]$.
  \item Backpropagate through soft ancestor assignments to update $\phi, \theta$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What the Guarantee Says}

The bias bound $O(e^{-c/\tau})$ is stronger than the $O(\tau)$ or
polynomial rates of OT-based alternatives. At temperature $\tau = 0.01$,
the bias is essentially zero for practical purposes, while gradients
remain well-defined through the sigmoid.

\section{Experimental Findings}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item On \textbf{Lorenz-96 stochastic dynamics}, DSR matches OT-based
    differentiable resampling on filtering NLL with \textbf{3--5$\times$
    lower} per-step wall-clock time.
  \item On \textbf{IAM handwriting generation}, DSR improves
    log-likelihood over both multinomial and OT-based resampling.
  \item The absence of iterative solvers is confirmed as the primary
    source of the speed advantage.
\end{itemize}

\section{Ablations and Interpretation}

\textbf{Without Morton sorting:} The soft ancestor matrix loses its banded
structure; gradient variance increases and ELBO estimates degrade.

\textbf{Temperature sensitivity:} An optimal $\tau^*$ balances gradient
noise (low $\tau$) vs.\ bias (high $\tau$); a short sweep identifies it.

\textbf{Number of particles:} The advantage over multinomial resampling
grows with $K$, consistent with the $O(K^{-2})$ vs.\ $O(K^{-1})$ variance
rates.

\textbf{Stop-gradient comparison:} Stop-gradient introduces a systematic
ELBO bias that DSR eliminates.
