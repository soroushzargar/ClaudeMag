Omni-modal large language models --- models that jointly process audio,
video, and text --- are increasingly used in real-time deployments.
Their KV cache grows proportionally to all modalities simultaneously:
a 7B omni-model processing 10 minutes of audio-video accumulates
$\sim$30\,GB of FP16 KV cache. 2-bit quantization would reduce this
to 4\,GB, enabling consumer deployment. But applying existing
rotation-based quantization methods (designed for text-only LLMs)
to multimodal caches cuts performance by 20 points or more.
OmniKVQuant diagnoses why --- two failure modes specific to multimodal
caches --- and fixes them with targeted, training-free interventions.

\section{KV Cache Memory in Omni-LLMs}

The KV cache size scales as:
\[
  M_{\mathrm{KV}} = 2 \cdot n_l \cdot n_h \cdot L \cdot d_h \cdot \text{prec}
\]
For a 7B omni-model (32 layers, 32 heads, $d_h = 128$) processing
10 minutes of audio-video at 25\,fps ($L \approx 15{,}000$), this is
$\approx$30\,GB at FP16 --- far beyond consumer GPU memory.

\textbf{Existing KV cache quantization} for text-only LLMs uses
rotation-based methods: apply an orthogonal matrix $R$ to key/value
vectors before quantization to eliminate outliers:
\[
  \tilde{\mathbf{V}} = R\mathbf{V}, \quad
  \hat{\mathbf{V}} = Q_2(\tilde{\mathbf{V}})
\]
where $Q_2$ is 2-bit rounding. $R$ is calibrated once on text
calibration data. On text-only LLMs, this reduces memory to
$\approx$4\,GB with $<$1 point degradation. On omni-modal caches,
applying the same $R$ causes 19--24 point degradation.

\section{Failure Mode 1: Temporal Key Drift}

In a text-only LLM, the statistical distribution of key vectors is
roughly stationary over the sequence. In an omni-modal model
processing audio:
\[
  \mathbf{K}_t = W_K \cdot \mathbf{x}_t^\text{audio}
\]
where $\mathbf{x}_t^\text{audio}$ is the audio embedding at time $t$.
Audio embeddings have strong temporal autocorrelation --- consecutive
frames are highly similar --- so the key distribution drifts
systematically over time:
\[
  \|\mathbf{K}_t - \mathbf{K}_{t-1}\|_2 \ll \|\mathbf{K}_t\|_2
\]
A quantization range $[r_\mathrm{min}, r_\mathrm{max}]$ calibrated at
initialization clips new key values that drift outside this range,
producing growing quantization error over longer audio streams.

\textbf{Fix: per-window key quantization.} Apply separate quantization
ranges to each temporal window of size $w$:
\[
  r_\mathrm{min}^{(t)} = \min_{t' \in [t-w,t]} K_{j,t'}^\mathrm{min},
  \quad
  r_\mathrm{max}^{(t)} = \max_{t' \in [t-w,t]} K_{j,t'}^\mathrm{max}
\]
This adapts the quantization range locally, tracking drift without
requiring global statistics. Window size $w = 64$ works well across
all tested models.

\section{Failure Mode 2: Heterogeneous Value Geometry}

The optimal rotation $R^*_m$ for modality $m$ is the PCA rotation of
the value covariance matrix $\Sigma_m$:
\[
  R^*_m \approx U_m^\top, \quad \Sigma_m = U_m \Lambda_m U_m^\top
\]
These matrices are modality-specific:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textit{Text:} Near-isotropic; $\sim$30 directions with
    $>$1\% variance.
  \item \textit{Audio:} Concentrated; 90\% variance in 5 low-frequency
    pitch/timbre directions.
  \item \textit{Video:} High-rank anisotropic; large dynamic range in
    spatial-frequency directions.
\end{itemize}

Applying $R^*_\text{text}$ to audio values leaves 85\% of variance
concentrated in 5 directions, producing massive outliers in the
rotated space that 2-bit quantization cannot faithfully represent.

\textbf{Fix: per-modality value rotation.} Calibrate and store
separate rotations $\{R^*_m\}$ per modality. At inference, detect
the current modality from the token type embedding and apply the
appropriate rotation:
\[
  \tilde{\mathbf{V}} = R^*_m \mathbf{V}, \quad
  \hat{\mathbf{V}} = Q_2(\tilde{\mathbf{V}})
\]

\section{Fused Triton Decode Kernel}

Standard 2-bit implementations dequantize the cache to FP16 before
attention, reconstructing the full dense cache and negating half the
memory savings. OmniKVQuant provides a fused Triton kernel that
dequantizes on the fly during attention computation, reading 2-bit
integers per block and expanding to FP16 without storing the
FP16 cache. Memory footprint remains at 2 bits throughout.

\textbf{2-bit symmetric quantization:}
\[
  \hat{v}_j = s \cdot \mathrm{round}\!\left(\frac{v_j}{s}\right),
  \quad \mathrm{round}(\cdot) \in \{-2,-1,0,1\},
  \quad s = \frac{\max_j |v_j|}{2}
\]

\section{Experimental Results}

Tested on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B and Qwen3-Omni-7B across 7 audio-visual
benchmarks (LibriSpeech, MMAU, Video-MME, ActivityNet-QA,
MSVD-QA, OmniBench, Evo-OMNIBench):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Method & Bits & Avg Score & $\Delta$ \\
\midrule
Full FP16           & 16 & 72.4 & --- \\
TurboQuant          & 2  & 48.3 & $-$24.1 \\
KIVI                & 2  & 52.7 & $-$19.7 \\
\textbf{OmniKVQuant} & \textbf{2} & \textbf{70.1} & $-$2.3 \\
OmniKVQuant         & 4  & 72.0 & $-$0.4 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

OmniKVQuant reduces 2-bit degradation from 19--24 points (prior methods)
to 2.3 points, and achieves near-lossless 4-bit quantization (0.4 points).

\section{Ablations and Interpretation}

\textbf{Without per-window key quantization:} Degradation grows from
2.3 to 11.4 points; confirms temporal key drift is the dominant
failure mode.

\textbf{Without per-modality value rotation:} Degradation grows from
2.3 to 8.9 points; audio benchmarks are disproportionately
affected ($-$18.3 on MMAU).

\textbf{Kernel overhead:} The fused Triton kernel adds 4\% latency
vs.\ FP16 attention while delivering 7.8$\times$ memory reduction
for 2-bit vs.\ FP16 caches.

\textbf{Window size:} $w = 64$ works well; smaller $w$ slightly
improves accuracy at the cost of more quantization parameter updates.
