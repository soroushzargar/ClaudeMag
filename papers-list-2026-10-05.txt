1. The Missing Primitive: Diagnosing and Repairing Mathematical Reasoning in Large Language Models
   Authors: Shuo Xing, Zilin Dai, Chengyuan Qian, Fangzhou Lin, Wenjing Chen, Ping He, Pan Lu, Alvaro Velasquez, Mohit Bansal, Zhengzhong Tu
   Affiliation: Texas A&M University; UNC Chapel Hill; UCLA; University of Colorado Denver
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.02191
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02191
   Summary: LLMs display impressive solving accuracy on frontier math problems while potentially lacking structural
   mathematical understanding. This paper introduces the concept of Mathematical Primitive—a concise, non-procedural
   representation of the foundational idea organizing a solution—and proposes the Prim benchmark spanning four
   complementary dimensions: Discovery, Generation, Digestion, and Execution. Diagnosis reveals that similar
   solving accuracy can mask markedly different capability profiles, correct primitives unlock substantial latent
   execution capacity, and Independent Discovery is the dominant bottleneck to mathematical reasoning. Leveraging
   these findings, a primitive-guided post-training procedure yields consistent improvements across multiple LLMs.

2. Error-Corrected Inference-Time Scaling for Imperfect Diffusion Models
   Authors: Zuokai Wen, Louis Grenioux, Weinan E, Jiequn Han
   Affiliation: Princeton University; Flatiron Institute, Simons Foundation
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.01933
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01933
   Summary: Inference-time scaling for diffusion models relies on Monte Carlo sampling with more particles but
   assumes the pretrained model is exact—a condition violated in practice by data and training imperfections.
   The paper introduces the Energy-based Feynman-Kac Corrector (EBFKC), a framework that derives Feynman-Kac
   dynamics capable of tracking a prescribed probability path exactly in the continuous-time population limit
   even when the model is imperfect, and approximates these dynamics using sequential Monte Carlo with
   variance-controlling guidance. Evaluated on Gaussian mixture models, particle systems, alanine dipeptide,
   and alanine tetrapeptide under annealing and reward tilting, EBFKC closely matches target distributions
   and molecular free-energy profiles whereas standard inference-time scaling baselines retain substantial
   sampling errors.

3. Foundations without Fundamentals: Zero-Shot Blind Spots in Time Series Foundation Models
   Authors: Nafiseh Ghoroghchian, Haipeng Zhang, Shuyi Han, Alex Labach, George Stein
   Affiliation: ServiceNow Research; McGill University
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.02058
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02058
   Summary: Despite impressive benchmark performance, Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) may lack the inductive
   biases needed to capture simple predictable functions. The paper introduces SimpleTimeBench, a diagnostic unit-test
   suite for temporal primitives such as monotonic trends, periodic signals, and leading-indicator covariates—scenarios
   where near-perfect zero-shot forecasts should be trivial. Testing prominent multivariate TSFMs (Chronos-2, Moirai,
   Toto), the paper finds they frequently produce suboptimal zero-shot forecasts on these simple inputs, revealing
   a gap between pre-training scale and basic temporal reasoning. These failures persist in real-world sensor
   forecasting where TSFMs consistently underutilize available leading indicators.

4. Beyond Linear Concepts: Discovering and Aligning Non-Linear Concept Manifolds in Large Language Models
   Authors: Tido Specht, Elias Benedict Krey, Nils Neukirch, Nils Strodthoff
   Affiliation: University of Oldenburg; German Research Center for AI (DFKI)
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.01821
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01821
   Summary: Existing mechanistic interpretability methods extract concepts from LLMs under a strong linearity
   assumption challenged by evidence of non-linear feature manifolds. This paper adapts Non-Linear Multi-Dimensional
   Concept Discovery (NLMCD) from computer vision to token-level LLM activations, modeling concepts as low-dimensional
   manifolds rather than linear directions. A new Concept-Based Alignment (CBA) score—a generalized Rand index
   measuring geometric proximity without explicit feature matching—reveals that alignment matrices exhibit block
   structures consistent across models, with representations remaining syntax-dominated through most of the
   network before giving way to increasingly mixed syntactic-semantic concepts in later layers.

5. TACO: Ternary Absolute-max Column-wise One-sparse Optimizer for LLM Fine-Tuning
   Authors: Jichao Jiang, Cristian McGee, El Houcine Bergou, Hanqin Cai, Aritra Dutta
   Affiliation: University of Central Florida; Mohammed VI Polytechnic University (UM6P)
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.02199
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02199
   Summary: Full-parameter fine-tuning of LLMs incurs substantial optimizer state memory overhead. TACO computes
   the exact steepest-descent direction under a dimension-normalized 1→1 operator norm by selecting the sign of
   the largest-magnitude entry in each column of two-dimensional weight matrices, following Muon's operator-norm
   view but pushing the geometric route further. TACO reduces persistent optimizer state by 174× relative to
   AdamW8bit (from 27.7 GB to 0.16 GB) and peak training memory by 2.9× (from 80.6 GB to 27.5 GB) on OPT-13B,
   while achieving comparable accuracy and runtime. TACO enables full-parameter fine-tuning of 30–32B parameter
   models on a single 80 GB H100 GPU across multiple model families and tasks.
