1. SAPO: Single-Rollout Autoregressive Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Dayang Liang, Lang Feng, Bo An, Yunlong Liu
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.19842
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19842
   Summary: Agentic reinforcement learning for LLMs faces a memory-compute dilemma: methods
   like PPO require a separate critic model, doubling memory, while group-relative methods like
   GRPO avoid a critic but provide only sequence-level credit and depend on large rollout
   groups. SAPO resolves both problems by exploiting the autoregressive structure of LLMs to
   produce policy and value predictions at distinct causal boundaries within a single shared
   backbone, independently optimizing PPO objectives and auxiliary on-policy SARSA objectives.
   A trajectory-level generalized advantage estimator combines lambda-returns with batch
   normalization to stabilize training on long-horizon tasks. On ALFWorld and WebShop with
   Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B, SAPO outperforms PPO and GRPO by mean +15.1 and +12.1 percentage
   points respectively, while eliminating the separate critic memory cost and reducing
   per-iteration runtime by 33.2% over PPO.

2. tau0-VLA: a Hierarchical Robot Foundation Model with World-Model-Guided Test-Time Computation [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: (SII Research team)
   Affiliation: SII Research
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.16885
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16885
   Summary: Long-horizon robot manipulation requires reliable skill execution and coherent
   subtask sequencing over extended trajectories. Most hierarchical vision-language-action
   (VLA) models make high-level decisions with a single forward pass, leaving no mechanism to
   allocate additional computation to ambiguous or high-stakes choices. tau0-VLA addresses this
   by formulating high-level subtask generation as a compute-scalable inference problem: a
   memory-augmented high-level policy monitors token-confidence statistics, and when confidence
   is low it triggers a propose-predict-evaluate loop in which the proposal model generates
   candidate subtasks, a world model predicts terminal observations, and a value model scores
   outcomes. Trained on 40,115 hours of heterogeneous real-world manipulation data, allocating
   additional test-time computation substantially improves next-subtask prediction accuracy,
   with gains directly translating to higher closed-loop success on long-horizon tasks.

3. Le Critique: Privileged Value Functions for LLM Reinforcement Learning [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Siddarth Venkatraman, Matthieu Dinot, Laurence Aitchison
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.16739
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16739
   Summary: Group-relative methods like GRPO reduce gradient variance in LLM reinforcement
   learning by sampling multiple rollouts per prompt, but they provide only sequence-level
   credit assignment and are bottlenecked by straggler rollouts that reduce throughput and
   increase off-policyness. Learned value functions theoretically address both problems, but
   accurate value estimation from the policy's own context is difficult for LLMs. Le Critique
   proposes Privileged Value Functions (PVF), which give the critic access to information
   beyond the standard token context (such as partial ground-truth or privileged task state)
   during training only, enabling more accurate token-level advantage estimates without
   contaminating the policy's inference context. The paper also introduces TETHER, an adaptive
   baseline that smoothly interpolates between group-relative and value-based advantages
   depending on critic reliability.

4. ComplexityWorld: Benchmarking Vision-Language Models on Verifiable Visual Decision Making [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Not specified
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.07584
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07584
   Summary: Vision-language models have made rapid progress in visual perception and
   increasingly support real-world tasks requiring images. Many such tasks demand more than
   recognizing image contents: a model must use visual evidence to make a complete decision
   whose components jointly satisfy global constraints. ComplexityWorld introduces a benchmark
   of 390 tasks across 39 domain-inspired visual worlds and 29 decision categories. Each task
   is generated from a hidden structured specification, rendered as a visual scene, and scored
   by an executable verifier that accepts any feasible solution. Under direct inference, all
   evaluated models except GPT-5.6-Sol remain below 40% verifier acceptance rate (VAR), while
   GPT-5.6-Sol reaches 75.6%. Performance improves substantially when decision information is
   made explicit in structured form, but varies sharply across equivalent visual presentations,
   revealing that current VLMs struggle to reliably extract and satisfy visual constraints.

5. How Do VLMs Behave When Blind or Misled? Behavioral Evaluation of VLMs on Scientific Figures [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Paul Osemudiame Oamen, Owusu-Banahene Osei, Ananya Mukherjee, Christian Greisinger,
            Steffen Eger, Pius Onobhayedo, Wei Zhao
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.13267
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13267
   Summary: Existing VLM benchmarks emphasize perception and reasoning accuracy while giving
   limited attention to behavioral reliability under uncertainty — how models behave when visual
   evidence is missing or misleading. This paper introduces SciFigBench, a diagnostic benchmark
   for scientific figure understanding that jointly evaluates perception, reasoning, and
   behavioral reliability. The benchmark contains 250 figures with high-quality human
   annotations across three evaluation aspects (600+ annotation hours) and extends these via
   image transformations, reasoning questions, resistance probes, caption-bias probes, and
   selective-blur targets, producing over 34,000 evaluation setups. Findings reveal that VLMs
   frequently confabulate answers when visual information is blurred or absent, and are
   systematically biased by figure captions even when captions contradict the visual content,
   raising serious reliability concerns for scientific AI assistants.
