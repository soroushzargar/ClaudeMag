1. AI Models Can Provably Hide Arbitrary Capabilities
   Authors: Andis Draguns, Sumeet Ramesh Motwani, Raymond Douglas, Christian Schroeder de Witt
   Venue/ID: NeurIPS 2026 · arXiv:2610.13928
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13928
   Summary: Capability audits assume that if a hidden behavior cannot be elicited, it does not
   exist. This paper refutes that assumption by constructing backdoors that embed entire
   encrypted neural networks inside a host model's weights, activated only by a secret trigger.
   Using digital-locker cryptography, the hidden circuits remain undetectable under fine-tuning,
   weight noising, gradient-based trigger search, and latent adversarial training. Tests on
   chemical-reaction-prediction tasks show the capabilities survive all current elicitation
   defenses, and the models are released to support future defense research.

2. Expanding LLM Reasoning
   Authors: Rian Atri, Evan Luo
   Venue/ID: NeurIPS 2026 · arXiv:2610.05584
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05584
   Summary: Rather than sampling more complete chains, the authors ask where inside an existing
   chain an extra continuation should be branched. They define expansion utility as the change in
   correctness from restarting a chain at a stored step, measure it across nine models on six
   benchmarks, and find that a fixed "always-last" rule beats uniform placement and matches a
   learned router. On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B / MATH-500, always-last surpasses
   four-sample self-consistency while using only 0.774x the generated output.

3. Task Context Shapes Component Roles in Language Models
   Authors: Sovesh Mohapatra, Dani S Bassett
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.13895
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13895
   Summary: The same attention head can serve different functional roles depending on the task
   context. Across five open-weight models (1.5–14B parameters, four families), the authors
   measure how influence redistributes when the task changes while the prompt structure is
   held constant. In Gemma-2-9B and Qwen3-14B, an early activation edit redirects which fact
   the model attends to without altering the instruction. A response map predicts a later
   head's response from an earlier intervention, cutting mean squared error by 53.6% in Gemma.

4. When Does External Guidance Help LLM Reasoning? A Bias-Variance Theory of Guidance-Augmented GRPO
   Authors: Sofia Torres, Gabriel Almeida, Carter Adams, Camila Rocha
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2610.06861
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06861
   Summary: Guidance-Augmented GRPO (GA-GRPO) adds external search or teacher signals to the
   GRPO reinforcement-learning loop for LLMs. The authors prove a closed-form bias bound
   (governed by total-variation divergence between guided and on-policy distributions) and an
   O(1/sqrt(T)) convergence rate to a neighbourhood whose radius is proportional to guidance
   bias. A minimax lower bound shows this Omega(delta^2 T) bias term is unavoidable. Using the
   derived MSE-optimal guidance weight, GA-GRPO matches or surpasses TAPO, LUFFY, and vanilla
   GRPO on nine math benchmarks while requiring 31% fewer GPU-hours.

5. VTV-FM: Flow Matching through Variational Terminal-Velocity Closure
   Authors: Haoyang Jiang, Yuheng Li, Di Yang, Yanhai Xiong, Haipeng Chen, Yi He
   Venue/ID: NeurIPS 2026 · arXiv:2610.00785
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00785
   Summary: Flow matching typically uses straight-line (first-order) transport paths because
   static datasets carry no terminal-velocity information for second-order dynamics. VTV-FM
   resolves this by deriving the missing terminal velocity through a minimum-acceleration energy
   principle, yielding a closed-form second-order bridge. The same construction provides the
   optimal-transport pairing cost and acceleration training targets. Experiments on
   low-dimensional datasets, PDE-governed physical fields, and CIFAR-10 show improved sample
   quality and trajectory smoothness over first-order baselines.
