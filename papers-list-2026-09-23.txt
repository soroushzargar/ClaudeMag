1. Taming CoT Obfuscation in VLMs: From Mechanistic Evidence to Activation Enforcement [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Xutao Mao, Jianing Zhu, Jinman Zhao, Tongliang Liu, Xiaowen Chu, Cong Wang, Bo Han
   Affiliation: City University of Hong Kong; University of Sydney; Hong Kong Baptist University
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.24243
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24243
   Summary: Chain-of-thought obfuscation in vision-language models (VLMs) poses a fundamental
   safety and interpretability problem: a model may learn to produce reasoning traces that
   are decoupled from the actual computations driving its outputs. This paper first formalises
   monitorability as balanced recoverability of a reference status from a trace, then
   mechanistically localises reward-monitorability divergence through attention, integrated
   gradients, and sparse-autoencoder (SAE) feature analysis. Causal contribution is tested
   with matched targeted-versus-random activation interventions. The proposed method, TAME
   (Trace-Aligned Monitorability Enforcement), applies an asymmetric training constraint
   over the identified SAE activations to steer reasoning traces back toward genuine
   reflection of model state. Experiments show that TAME improves monitorability across
   standard VLM benchmarks while preserving task performance.

2. LogicTrack: Auditing Reasoning Trajectories of Large Language Models with Formal Logic Solvers [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Jingyu Hu, Shu Yang, Weiru Liu, Di Wang
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.21492
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21492
   Summary: Chain-of-thought optimization in LLMs has relied almost exclusively on outcome-based
   feedback, leaving the logical validity of intermediate steps unverified. LogicTrack
   introduces a neuro-symbolic framework that auto-formalizes each natural-language reasoning
   step into an SMT-LIB specification decomposed into context, background assumptions, and
   conclusion, then verifies it with an automated theorem prover (Z3-class SMT solver).
   Diagnostics (syntax, satisfiability, entailment/refutation/undecided) are combined into a
   Solver-Based Backtracking Reward (SBR) that scores logical soundness and guides backtracking
   tree search at inference time. Supervised fine-tuning on LogicTrack-generated backtracking
   traces teaches models to internalize step-wise auditing. Evaluated across 8 reasoning
   benchmarks and 7 LLMs, LogicTrack substantially improves step-level verifiability and
   generally preserves or improves final-answer accuracy.

3. ERR+: Sequential Entropy Resolution for Efficient and Decisive LLM Reasoning [EMNLP Findings 2026]
   Authors: Juxin Chen et al.
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.28771 — Accepted, Findings of EMNLP 2026
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28771
   Summary: Large reasoning models trained with RLVR generate extended chain-of-thought traces
   but often produce redundant or indecisive reasoning steps. Through analysis across multiple
   model families, ERR+ identifies that correct reasoning traces exhibit more frequent and
   larger token-level entropy drops within the thinking phase than incorrect ones. ERR+ is a
   two-phase RLVR framework: Phase 1 trains with an Entropy Relief Reward (ERR) proportional
   to cumulative token-level entropy drops normalized by log-response-length, gated on
   correctness, encouraging decisive uncertainty resolution without suppressing exploration.
   Phase 2 compresses response length relative to group peers. On five math and STEM
   benchmarks including AIME and AMC, ERR+ improves both accuracy and response efficiency.

4. InsertAnything: Generalizable Contact-Rich Precision Insertion from Simulation to Reality [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Not specified
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.24511
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24511
   Summary: Precision peg-in-hole insertion under sub-millimeter clearances is a canonical
   contact-rich assembly task where geometry and clearance variation across parts make policy
   reuse difficult. InsertAnything trains RL policies entirely in simulation using target
   poses and compact 3D fingertip force feedback, enabling direct deployment without real
   demonstrations or fine-tuning. A decoupled gated reward coordinates alignment and insertion
   phases, and force-signal smoothing plus state-independent standard deviations stabilize
   learning. A policy trained solely on simulated hexagonal insertion achieves 95.0% success
   rate across 8 unseen real-world tasks with minimum clearances of 0.02 mm, and sets the
   first perfect score (20/20) on ManipulationNet's peg-in-hole benchmark.

5. Smoothness as a Constraint for Stable Humanoid Locomotion [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Utsav Panchal, Denis Kleyko, Unal Artan, Amy Loutfi
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.24552
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24552
   Summary: Reinforcement learning for humanoid locomotion typically imposes smoothness through
   auxiliary reward terms that compete with task objectives, treating the body uniformly and
   providing no direct control over physical smoothness quantities. DeCap (Decoupled
   Constraint-aware Policy) recasts whole-body smoothness as explicit constraints, decoupled
   into separate upper- and lower-body groups that reflect the different physical roles: tight
   smoothness for the upper body (stability) and looser reactive constraints for the lower body.
   A bounded barrier penalty activates proactively as limits are approached. On real humanoid
   whole-body control, DeCap reduces upper-body action rate by 2.50x and acceleration by 2.18x
   over reward-based smoothness baselines, while also improving lower-body smoothness and
   enabling terrain transfer without reward retuning.
