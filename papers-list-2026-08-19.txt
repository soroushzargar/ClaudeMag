1. Fewer Tokens, Smaller Cache: Reward-Coordinated Efficient Reasoning [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Qiyuan Zhu, Dezhi Li, Pengyu Cheng, Tianle Chen, Jiacheng Wang, Ruijie Shen,
            Hao Gu, Sida Lin, Zirui Liu, Jiacheng Liu, Sirui Han
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.04771
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04771
   Summary: Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) excel on complex tasks through long chain-of-thought
   reasoning, but their lengthy intermediate steps cause severe overthinking that inflates
   inference cost. KV-cache compression is a common remedy, yet existing approaches apply a
   uniform policy across the entire trajectory and evaluate compression solely by what is
   removed from the cache. This paper makes two observations: (1) a reasoning state's
   tolerance to context loss varies along the trajectory, and process reward tracks this
   variation — deleting tokens at high-reward steps preserves accuracy far better than random
   deletion; (2) compression is not free on the generation side, since a smaller cache causes
   the model to generate more tokens, partly canceling the saving. The paper introduces
   Reward-Coordinated Efficient Reasoning (RCER), which jointly coordinates both sides under a
   single process reward signal to simultaneously reduce tokens generated and cache size.

2. Towards Physics of Multimodal Pretraining: Knowledge Flow, Modality Synergy, Early Unification, and Recipes [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Junlin Han, Shengbang Tong, David Fan, Minghao Chen, Philip Torr,
            Filippos Kokkinos, Mike Lewis
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.05000
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05000
   Summary: Despite momentum in multimodal pretraining, the design space and fundamental
   mechanisms of how modalities interact during unified training remain underexplored. This
   paper provides empirical clarity through systematic exploration and identifies four key
   findings: (1) Language, visual understanding, and visual generation transfer knowledge
   across modalities with distinct asymmetric patterns; (2) Data complexity largely determines
   whether modalities are synergistic, with shared attention and normalization layers (with
   modality-specific feed-forward layers) promoting synergy; (3) Unifying modalities from
   early stages is more effective than late alignment or sequential training, uncovering a
   "vision laziness" phenomenon where delayed integration causes models to rely on language
   priors; (4) Efficient pretraining recipes achieve strong generative performance using only
   5% of the compute budget, validated by training multiple 13.5B MoE models on 2T tokens.

3. XYZFlow: Scaling Multidimensional Shortcut Flows for Efficient Generative Modeling [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Jinxiu Liu, Xuanming Liu, Kangfu Mei, Yandong Wen, Weiyang Liu
   Affiliation: CUHK; Westlake University; Johns Hopkins University
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.12276
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12276
   Summary: High-fidelity image generation faces a fundamental trade-off between speed and
   quality. Diffusion models produce strong visuals but require costly iterative sampling;
   existing efficient methods mainly distill pretrained models into few-step samplers, a
   challenging process that depends heavily on teacher-model quality. XYZFlow rethinks
   efficient generation through multidimensional scaling of flow matching. Unlike single-step
   mappings, XYZFlow enhances expressivity by making probability paths more identifiable and
   learnable through structured multidimensional conditioning using both temporal (denoising
   history) and spatial (sequential patch) dimensions. The paper shows that autoregressive
   modeling can be viewed as implicit flow straightening, where richer context reduces
   trajectory ambiguity and enables high-quality generation in fewer function evaluations.

4. Sparse Prototype Code Underlies Classification and Prediction Across Modalities [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Yehonatan Avidan, Daniel D. Lee, Haim Sompolinsky
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.15632
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15632
   Summary: Neural representations have become a central tool for studying the internal
   mechanisms of modern AI models, yet their complex high-dimensional structure makes them
   difficult to interpret. This paper discovers that classification tasks give rise to a
   universal representational geometry, shared across state-of-the-art models in vision,
   audio, and language processing. The key structure is that within-class variability is not
   random in representation space — its classifier-relevant component has strong and structured
   correlations with the class's own centroid and with the centroids of competing classes.
   The paper characterizes this geometry as a "Sparse Prototype Code" and shows that it is
   shared across diverse architectures and modalities, providing a unified framework for
   understanding why deep networks generalize and what distinguishes high-performing
   representations.

5. Temporal GRPO: Beyond Trajectory-Level Credit in Vision-Language-Action Reinforcement Learning [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Yao Zhou, Hang Gao, Fengge Wu, Changwen Zheng, Wenwen Qiang
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.13026
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13026
   Summary: Reinforcement learning for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models typically assigns
   credit at the trajectory level — the entire action sequence receives the same reward signal.
   This coarse assignment fails to identify which specific actions within a trajectory
   contributed to success or failure, leading to slow convergence and suboptimal policies.
   Temporal GRPO extends the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm with a
   temporal credit assignment mechanism that decomposes trajectory-level rewards into
   step-level credit signals. By conditioning credit assignment on the temporal context of
   each action within the manipulation sequence, Temporal GRPO provides more informative
   gradient signals to the VLA policy, improving sample efficiency and final performance on
   robotic manipulation benchmarks.
