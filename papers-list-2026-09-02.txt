1. How Much Rank Does LoRA Need? Rank-Error Bounds for Transformer Attention [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Gerard Conangla Planes
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.26052
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26052
   Summary: Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) is the dominant paradigm for fine-tuning large language
   models, yet practitioners choose the rank r by heuristic. This paper gives the first
   task-dependent theory of LoRA approximation error for Transformer attention: fixing a
   pretrained attention head, a target attention function, and a downstream input distribution,
   it bounds the minimum expected KL divergence achievable by a rank-r query LoRA update.
   The key quantity is the tail energy T_r of the target update matrix under the
   downstream-weighted spectral decomposition. The paper proves lower and upper bounds on
   the best rank-r KL error as explicit functions of T_r, and shows that softmax saturation
   can make the rank required to match an attention function strictly smaller than the rank
   needed to match the logits—with a constant-factor separation for an explicit construction.
   The results extend to fused multi-head LoRA and joint query/key updates, giving
   practitioners a principled basis for rank selection.

2. Stay Within Your Bounds: Distance-Guided Decoding for Guaranteed Context-Free Grammar Compliance [arXiv 2026 / EMNLP 2026 Findings]
   Authors: Vincenzo Collura, Karim Tit, Eleonora Giunchiglia, Mike Papadakis, Maxime Cordy
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.28229 — EMNLP 2026 Findings (Long Paper)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28229
   Summary: Grammar-constrained decoding ensures that language model outputs satisfy a
   context-free grammar (CFG), enabling reliable generation of structured artifacts such as
   JSON, SQL, and linear temporal logic (LTL) formulas. Existing methods suffer from
   incomplete paths: under tokenizer-grammar mismatch, feasible prefixes can steer into
   regions from which no complete valid string is reachable. This paper proposes a
   lookahead-guided decoding framework based on pushdown automata (PDA) summaries that
   compute bounded upper-bound distances to the nearest accepting state for each reachable
   PDA configuration. These distance signals are used online to prune tokens that cannot
   lead to acceptance within the remaining token budget and to guide beam search toward
   acceptance. Every output generated by the resulting decoder is guaranteed to be accepted
   by the target grammar. Experiments on JSON, SQL, and LTL structured generation show
   consistent syntactic validity and improved completion quality over prior baselines.

3. Focus Where It Counts: A Salience-Driven Vision-Language Model for Low Vision Assistance [arXiv 2026 / ECCV 2026]
   Authors: Jiazhao Liang, Hao Huang, Shuaihang Yuan, et al. (11 authors total)
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.28218 — ECCV 2026
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28218
   Summary: Vision-language models offer powerful captioning capabilities but are designed
   for general-purpose use and do not explicitly model perceptual priorities relevant to
   persons with blindness or low vision. This paper introduces a salience-driven captioning
   framework for assistive technology. The authors curate three salience-aware datasets
   (Salience COCO, Salience Flickr, Salience VizWiz) with object-level salience annotations
   representing human-centered visual priorities. They then train Salience-LLaVA, a
   salience-aware VLM that generates captions in which important scene elements are mentioned
   in order of their relevance to the user's task. The framework improves assistive caption
   quality by ensuring the most functionally relevant information appears early in the
   generated description, directly addressing the needs of users who may only receive
   partial information before interrupting or time-limiting the description.

4. Designing Reinforcement Learning for Diffusion Models: A Unified Path-Space View [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Yixian Xu, Yuanrui Zhang, Shengjie Luo, Liwei Wang, Di He
   Affiliation: Peking University; ByteDance Seed
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.14430
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14430
   Summary: Reinforcement learning post-training aligns diffusion models with task-specific
   rewards, but the existing landscape of RL algorithms for diffusion models is fragmented:
   reverse-trajectory methods use discretized likelihood ratios, while forward-matching
   methods train on reward-labeled noising versions, with no unified principle explaining
   or connecting them. This paper shows that both families arise from a single path-space
   importance-sampling principle applied to the diffusion SDE. Using importance sampling
   between sampling SDEs, the authors derive an explicit policy-gradient estimator on
   trajectory space and its equivalent variance-reduced value-gradient form. The derivation
   yields a unified design space organized along three axes: value-gradient estimation
   strategy, weight functions, and sampling choices. The authors propose a multi-sample
   KDE value-gradient estimator with scale-bounded weight families and validate the
   variance-reduction explanation on SD3.5-M and Qwen-Image, showing improvements over
   prior diffusion-RL baselines.

5. Robust CurveMoE: Multi-Norm Adversarial Defense for Mixture-of-Experts Models via Mode Connectivity [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Xu Zhang, Ren Wang
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.26043
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26043
   Summary: Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models are increasingly deployed at scale, but their
   robustness to adversarial perturbations—especially across multiple Lp-norm threat
   models simultaneously—remains poorly understood. This paper applies mode connectivity
   to the MoE adversarial robustness problem: training separate MoE models to be robust
   under individual Lp norms, then finding a low-loss path through weight space connecting
   these robust solutions and sampling a model from this path. The resulting CurveMoE
   model inherits multi-norm robustness from the endpoint models while maintaining task
   accuracy, avoiding the accuracy-robustness trade-off that afflicts single-model
   adversarial training. The paper shows that mode connectivity paths for MoE models
   remain low-loss and can transfer adversarial robustness properties, extending the
   mode connectivity paradigm from dense networks to the more complex routing-dependent
   MoE architecture.
