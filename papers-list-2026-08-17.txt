1. Answer First, Reason Later: Commitment Order in Diffusion LLMs [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Jewon Yeom, Jaewon Sok, Seonghyeon Park, Jeongjae Park, Hwiyeong Lee, Taesup Kim
   Affiliation: Seoul National University
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.05687
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05687
   Summary: Masked diffusion language models (dLLMs) promise order-free token generation as a
   core advantage over autoregressive decoding. This paper reveals that, on reasoning tasks,
   this freedom is the source of failure rather than strength. By logging every token commitment
   during decoding of LLaDA-8B on GSM8K, the authors show that unconstrained pure decoding
   commits the final answer at only 15–24% of the trajectory while half the reasoning region
   is still masked. As the canvas grows, the model collapses to answer-only outputs on up to
   90% of problems, skipping its own reasoning entirely. The paper introduces a constrained
   decoding strategy that enforces reasoning-before-answer commitment order, recovering
   substantial accuracy with minimal overhead.

2. ThinkRetrieve: Retrieval-Augmented Reasoning Traces for Test-Time Scaling [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Vaibhav Singh, Soumya Suvra Ghosal, Sarvesh Gharat, Soumyabrata Pal,
            Ramasuri Narayanam, Dinesh Manocha
   Affiliation: IIT Bombay; University of Maryland College Park; Adobe Research
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.10928
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10928
   Summary: Sequential test-time scaling of Large Reasoning Models frequently yields diminishing
   or negative returns: longer reasoning traces exhibit increased uncertainty, error compounding,
   and problem drift. ThinkRetrieve is a test-time framework that augments the reasoning traces
   of LRMs with dynamically retrieved solved examples at each intermediate reasoning step.
   Rather than injecting retrieved facts, it injects solved step-by-step solutions from an
   external corpus, providing the model with guidance on how to reason at the precise moment it
   needs it. Experiments across five reasoning models (1.5B–8B parameters) on GSM-8K, MATH-500,
   AIME 2025, and SciQ demonstrate consistent accuracy improvements over standard test-time
   scaling approaches.

3. Procedural Fairness Failures in RLHF from Preference Averaging [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: M P V S Gopinadh, Karthik Kamuju, Kummari Avinash, John Joshua,
            Srinivasa Raju Rudraraju
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.10126 — ICLR 2026 Workshop on Algorithmic Fairness Across
             Alignment Procedures and Agentic Systems (AFAA)
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10126
   Summary: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) aggregates heterogeneous
   human preferences into a single reward model, implicitly assuming preference homogeneity.
   When preferences are in fact heterogeneous, this aggregation induces a procedural fairness
   failure: majority preference groups dominate reward learning while minority preferences are
   systematically under-represented — even if the minority is individually coherent. The paper
   defines procedural fairness in alignment and shows that standard RLHF violates it through
   preference averaging. It proposes Preference-Aware RLHF (PA-RLHF), which separates
   optimization across preference modes at the reward learning stage to preserve distinct
   preference signals.

4. RoboSynChallenge: Mastering Real-World Dexterity via Generalizing Synthesized Manipulation Skills [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Runyi Zhao, Ruixin Wu, Chengkun Li, Hongrui Zhang, Ang Li, Ruixing Jin,
            Yueci Deng, Yingying Guo, Lihe Ding, Shaocong Dong, Tianfan Xue, Yanjun Gao,
            Yudong Luo, Pascal Poupart, Simo Wu, Kui Jia, Wei-shi Zheng, Guiliang Liu
   Affiliation: Multiple institutions
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.12416 — NeurIPS 2026 Competition Track
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12416
   Summary: Generalizable robotic manipulation is severely bottlenecked by the scarcity and
   narrow diversity of real-world demonstration data. RoboSynChallenge is a unified benchmark
   and competition that evaluates manipulation policy generalizability across tasks, environments,
   and difficulty levels. The challenge integrates large-scale synthetic data generation with
   standardized real-world robotic evaluation: participants train on synthesized state-action
   trials and are assessed exclusively on unseen real-world manipulation environments. This
   design deliberately tests the sim-to-real transfer and compositional generalization of
   learned policies beyond their training distribution.

5. SIRIN: A Unified Toolkit for Detecting Contextual Hallucinations in Retrieval-Augmented and Memory-Grounded LLM Systems [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Julia Belikova, Rauf Parchiev, Mikhail Filimonov, Konstantin Polev,
            Andrey Savchenko, Maksim Makarenko
   Affiliation: Not specified
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2608.00033
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00033
   Summary: Contextual hallucinations — fluent, plausible responses unsupported by the provided
   evidence — are a critical reliability failure in retrieval-augmented and memory-grounded LLM
   systems. SIRIN (Semantic Inconsistency Recognition and Inspection Nexus) is a unified toolkit
   and interactive web UI that consolidates three detector paradigms: representation probing,
   uncertainty estimation, and judge-style verification. It also addresses the complementary
   task of pre-generation query answerability under one interface, configuration system, and
   evaluation pipeline. SIRIN supports response- and span-level inspection in both white-box
   (access to model internals) and black-box (API-only) settings, enabling systematic
   benchmarking of hallucination detection methods.
