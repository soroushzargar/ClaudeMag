1. SAS: Simple Attention Sparsification via End-to-End Optimization of Context Ranking [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Zhiwei Li, Lei Zhu, Hao Gu, Xiang Hu, Yan Wang, Haitao Mi, Sirui Han,
            Leo Liang, Zhijiang Guo
   Affiliation: Tencent HY LLM Frontier; Hong Kong University of Science and Technology
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.13141
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13141
   Summary: Efficient inference for long-context Transformers requires reducing attention
   from quadratic to sub-linear by retaining only a sparse set of relevant KV entries.
   Existing trainable sparse attention methods use a lightweight context selector followed
   by a hard Top-K operation to select keys, but the non-differentiable Top-K blocks
   gradients and prevents end-to-end optimization with the language modeling objective.
   SAS (Simple Attention Sparsification) inserts continuous log-space gates into the
   attention softmax, transforming sparsification into a smooth optimization problem.
   A gate g_i = sigma(s_i) is injected as log g_i into the attention logits before
   softmax so that gradient flows from the language modeling loss back through the
   attention computation to the selector. Normalized softmax gates calibrate historical
   context against a mandatory current block. SAS outperforms trainable baselines on
   reasoning, long-context understanding, and agentic tasks under tight sparsity budgets.

2. Temporal Recurrence Favors Fewer Layers [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Ivan Anokhin, Johan Obando-Ceron, Irina Rish, Sebastian Risi
   Affiliation: Mila / Université de Montréal; University of Copenhagen
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.12531
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12531
   Summary: Deep learning lore holds that depth is critical for model expressivity, yet
   this intuition was developed for non-recurrent models that cannot accumulate
   computation across time steps. This paper studies how compute should be allocated
   across within-step depth, expert width, and the number of parallel experts for
   recurrent vs. non-recurrent models. Evaluated on Sokoban planning (a hard sequential
   reasoning task) and FineWeb autoregressive language modeling, the study finds a sharp
   difference: recurrent models peak at 2-4 layers, while non-recurrent models continue
   improving up to 8-32 layers. Under the same compute budget, recurrent architectures
   achieve their best performance with fewer, wider layers, suggesting that temporal
   recurrence and within-step depth provide partially overlapping computational benefits.

3. Rethinking Heterogeneous System Disaggregation for Subquadratic Attention [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Arya Tschand, Yaosheng Fu, Vikram Sharma Mailthody, Nicolai Oswald,
            Po-An Tsai, Ritchie Zhao, Oreste Villa, Vijay Janapa Reddi, Karu Sankaralingam
   Affiliation: Harvard University; NVIDIA; University of Wisconsin-Madison
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.13134
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13134
   Summary: Frontier LLMs increasingly replace full quadratic attention with subquadratic
   variants (sparse, linear, sliding-window) to reduce KV memory. Current heterogeneous
   hardware disaggregation strategies split workloads by operator type — prefill on one
   device, decode on another — but do not account for the distinct arithmetic intensity
   profile of subquadratic attention layers. This paper introduces SQD (SubQuadratic
   Disaggregation), a fine-grained scheme that disaggregates within the decode step
   itself. For sparse attention, SQD separates top-k key selection (full KV traversal,
   memory-bound) from top-k attention plus FFN (static memory, compute-bound). For
   linear and sliding-window attention, SQD separates dense attention layers from
   subquadratic layers and FFN. On an 8xB200 heterogeneous system, SQD achieves 53%
   improvement in tokens per joule on GLM 5.2, 31% on Nemotron 3 Ultra, and 56% on
   Gemma 4 31B compared with a GPU-only baseline.

4. UniMo: Unifying Human and Animal Motion Generation [SIGGRAPH Asia 2026]
   Authors: Zeyu Zhang, Zhiyuan Zhang, Siheng Wang, Yiran Wang, Danning Li,
            Ian Reid, Richard Hartley
   Affiliation: University of Adelaide; Australian National University
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.12342 — Accepted, SIGGRAPH Asia 2026
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12342
   Summary: Text-driven 3D motion generation for humans has matured rapidly, but animals
   remain largely out of scope because their skeletal topologies differ radically across
   species, preventing the standardization that human motion models rely on. UniMo
   addresses this by converting parametric skeletons to unparametric point cloud
   representations, removing dependence on topology and allowing a single model to handle
   humans and arbitrary animal species. Dynamic sampling allocates more point budget to
   actively moving joints. The paper also contributes UniML3D, a large-scale dataset of
   145,907 motion sequences and 433,388 text captions spanning human and animal
   categories — 102 times larger than the largest existing animal motion dataset. UniMo
   achieves state of the art on HumanML3D, KIT-ML, and AnimalML3D benchmarks.

5. Attention Quantization for Tabular Foundation Models [arXiv 2026]
   Authors: Jonas M. Kübler, Benjamin Jäger, Klemens Flöge, Noah Hollmann, Frank Hutter
   Affiliation: University of Freiburg; ELLIS Institute Tübingen
   Venue/ID: arXiv:2609.13031
   URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13031
   Summary: Tabular foundation models such as TabPFN and TabICL operate via in-context
   learning, encoding entire training datasets as KV pairs in a single forward pass. Their
   KV matrices scale quadratically with the number of training rows and can reach millions
   of entries, dwarfing the KV caches of generative LLMs. Standard FP8 attention
   quantization methods designed for LLMs fail on tabular FMs because misaligned
   quantization error between test and training rows degrades prediction accuracy. This
   paper applies FP8 (e4m3fn format) quantization with dynamic scale calibration to the
   attention computation, introduces an alignment procedure to equalize quantization error
   across test and training rows, and provides a custom Triton kernel that achieves 1.7x
   speedup over 16-bit baselines. No accuracy loss is observed on TabPFN-v3 and TabICLv2
   across TabArena and BeyondArena benchmarks.
